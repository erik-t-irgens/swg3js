# Lessons: Rendering

Three's traps: light counts, glass shadows, tone mapping and targets, the effects chain, motion blur, program keys, disposing materials, culling, the portal renderer, shadows, AO, flare, haze, depth of field, lights in rooms and the rooms' light pool.

Moved here from CLAUDE.md's "Things that took a long time to learn" on 2026-11-19, word for word and in their old order. Add a new lesson about this area here, never to CLAUDE.md. A code comment that cites "CLAUDE.md" or "the lesson in CLAUDE.md" means one of these files: grep `docs/lessons/` for its words.

- Changing the number of lights in the scene recompiles every shader (a second each on the owner's machine). Lights are pooled and never added or removed at runtime: `effects.flash` is a pool of point lights that everything borrows, including a ship's room lights.
- Materials with `transparent` true and any opacity still cast full shadows; glass meshes get `castShadow = false`. The converter marks glass-named shaders with `userData.glass`; the game never blends them (the client did not either), it swaps in a clear copy of the material while someone is in the ship.
- Three applies tone mapping only to the default framebuffer: with a render target bound the materials come out linear, so post-processing must end in an OutputPass, and toggling it recompiles every program (the tone mapping is in the program key). World warm-up binds `world.compileTarget()` (the effects' scene target) around every compile, so the programs built behind the loading screen are the ones drawn; switching Effects compiles the other variants in the background (`reconcileEffects`) and changes over when they are linked. Three's own output pass works its tone-mapping defines out on its first draw, so it is given one throw-away draw before the warm-up compiles it, or the program built there is one no frame ever uses. Every effect's settings, knobs, pass order, products and budgets live in `src/core/fxRegistry.ts`; `npm run test:fx` checks them.
- The scene draws into `postfx.sceneTarget`, which no effect writes into; the chain ping-pongs between two buffers with no depth. A pass may sample the scene's depth texture only while a target without that texture is bound: Chrome drops the draw otherwise (INVALID_OPERATION, and silently once its error cap is hit), which is why the god rays and the speed blur never drew before. Geometry products (heat, water mask, velocity) attach the same depth texture read-only, so they never sample it; a target sharing it must be the scene target's size, and is detached before being disposed on its own, since disposing a target disposes its depth texture. `__debug.fxCheck()` names a step that errors.
- Never give the effects' render target `samples`: three resolves a multisampled target at the end of every `render()` call, and the portal renderer makes a dozen calls a frame, so bloom alone dropped the frame from 144 to 11 a second. The target is single-sampled and FXAA smooths the edges.
- A NaN pixel from any material becomes, through the bloom's mip blur, a screen-sized black box whenever that object is in view (the owner found a plant on Tatooine that does it). The effects chain starts with a pass that zeroes NaN and Inf pixels; `__debug.blackBox()` scans the frame and names the object under the first bad pixel, which is how to find the material at fault.
- The motion blur merges two velocities per pixel. For everything still, the camera's reprojection of the scene depth, with a cut (25 to 60 m) pushed out past the far side of whatever the camera follows (`ctx.followFar`, from `App.followFar`), so the ship flown stays sharp at any size; last frame's view is taken with this frame's projection, so aiming's zoom is not motion. For movers, a velocity product drawn only for the objects `App.collectMovers` lists, and of those only the meshes that differ from the reprojection: what the camera follows draws only parts that move relative to it (wings opening, a weapon in hand); other movers draw everything while their root moves and only such parts while it stands. Limbs are not blurred on their own: no bone history is kept. The blur is a reconstruction filter (resolve, tile max, neighbour max, a depth-tested gather), so a sharp hull leaves no ghost in the ground behind it. **In the race view and a flown ship's chase what the camera follows is kept sharp by its own pixels, not the cut** (`masksFollowed`, `MOTION_TUNING.followRule` 'auto'; 'cut' is the old rule everywhere, 'mask' every view from outside; first person, the cockpit, a jump's tunnel and the editor's eye keep the cut whatever it says): the cut held the ground and walls right beside a pod, the fastest things on the screen, as sharp as the pod. The velocity product then draws the whole of what is followed (`classifyMover`'s `mask`, its own cap `maxMaskDraws`) with `uFollowed` 1, so its riding parts and its rider's skinned meshes store nought, and the cut shrinks to the lens (`maskCut`); the view is `App.placeCamera`'s word a frame (`followView`, through the frame context). **A mask with a hole is no mask**: a part first seen (not yet drawable), a variant building or failed, a stale scan, a follow over its cap, a fresh or jumped follow -- any of them and the frame keeps the cut (`wholeMask`, `rec.unready`, `rec.stale`), since a hole is the hull smeared by the camera's whole motion; the pass reads the product's verdict for the frame it is drawing (`maskedOn`). Under the mask what is followed is counted every frame (`countTree` against the scan's own count), so a part added between rescans is a hole that frame rather than a part nobody knows to smear, and a follow over `maxMaskDraws` is classified again by the old rule (`unmask`), so the cut frame draws its moving parts as b4bfa5c did instead of nothing. A glow sprite riding the follow writes no depth, so its pixels are the ground's: under the mask it is drawn as a camera-facing quad on three's own shared sprite quad (`placeGlow`, uploaded by the first sprite the scene drew) with the warm rigid alpha variant cut by its picture (`glowAlphaTest`); an engine trail is world-fixed samples and rightly takes the ground's motion; the hull's own shadow on the ground is one pixel with the ground and is streaked with it, which one velocity a pixel cannot avoid. The race view's scale is `blurScale`'s: its shutter over the frame with no hold (held to twice the frame, 1/60 read as 1/72 at 144 Hz and every Shutter past it moved nothing), the streak held by its K alone. The race view's K is its own (`raceRadiusOfHeight`, held to `MAX_TILE_K` 128, the tile loop's bound): the tile targets are sized once for the smaller of the two Ks and a frame draws into as much as its K needs (each target's `viewport`, three's own, and `uTiles`, which the neighbour max and the gather clamp to in place of `textureSize`), so entering the race view allocates nothing; its shutter, strength (times the setting's), K and taps are `motionTune`'s dials (`src/core/fx/motionDials.ts`).
- Velocity draws go through `renderBufferDirect` inside an empty proxy scene's `onAfterRender`, one material per program variant (fourteen warmed behind the loading screen; fighters, peers and new mobiles prepared before they show), and must never throw there (three's render state would stay pushed). A direct draw skips three's own checks, so each mesh is checked every frame as `projectObject` would (visible up to the scene, actor layer, frustum) and drawn only after it passed on an earlier frame: a mesh never projected has no buffers, and drawing it saves a broken VAO for good. Records are checked every frame for removal, material swaps and disposed geometry. A variant's first real draw must not work its program out again (`side` is in three's program key, and a double-sided mesh first would compile a second one mid-frame), so `compileVariant` records the material version as three does after its own first draw. Remote players are listed with the relay's smoothed velocity: their glide between messages pulses at ten a second. A new thing that moves on its own must be added to `collectMovers`, or it blurs only with the camera.
- **A material made after the loading screen must have the program key it will keep, from its first draw.** A blaster bolt's two materials are made per colour the first time that colour is fired, and the first frame draws them before the quarter-second scan adopts them; adopted afterwards they gained the cascades' defines and hook, which are in the key, so every colour after the warm-up's two built a program in play, and a room's crew firing a colour of their own built two under the room's lights (the "MeshBasicMaterial · 1 dir, 12 point" that walking into the corvette showed: traced by wrapping `createProgram` and the prototype's `onBeforeRender` in a driven tab, which names the mesh being drawn when a program is made). They are marked `unlit` and `dry` now, so no scan ever changes their key and the warm-up's two colours build the one program every colour draws with, in the world's pass and a room's. Anything else made lazily in play -- a colour, a beam, an effect -- is the same question.
- A material that is disposed must be taken out of the portal renderer's `Set` and the shadow cascades' `Map` (`World.forgetMaterials`); both are strong. The portal renderer's is the one that costs per frame -- it is walked once per stencil change, about a dozen times a frame -- and the cascades' is a leak that shows as a stutter when the shadow distance changes. A material must also join the cascades (`csm.setupMaterial`, inside `World.adoptMaterials`) before its program is compiled, because the defines and the `onBeforeCompile` are part of the program key; an unlit material (`userData.unlit`) is kept out of them altogether for the same reason in reverse.
- The colour grade (`src/core/fx/gradeMath.ts`, pass `colorGrade`) runs in linear HDR just before the OutputPass. Every tint keeps the pixel's luminance, and its contrast turns about 0.18 divided by the exposure and leaves anything below a thirty-second of that and above thirty-two times it untouched, so it cannot crush shadows or clip highlights; a raise in saturation is scaled by how grey the pixel already is, so strong colours never clip. A planet's mood (gloom, heat, warmth) is hand-set in `GRADE_LOOKS`, not read from the sky: a planet's environment blocks differ region by region (one planet's keys run from dim to bright), so a mood read from whichever block is in use is an accident of block order. The sky supplies only the hues and how hard the key stands against the fill. Rooms and ship interiors take 30% of it for the whole view, eased over a second both ways; an exits mask from the portal stencil was worked through on paper and dropped, because it dipped the street on the way out and flickered on the door plane. `__debug.gradeSelfTest()` checks the shader against the JavaScript mirror that `npm run test:grade` sweeps.
- A lit blade's light on what is around it is the `bladeGlow` effects pass: it rebuilds each pixel's view-space position and a normal from the scene depth and adds every blade as a segment light (inverse square with a soft core and a smooth knee, wrapped Lambert, the surface's hue, zero at 3.5 m), and the tuning is `BLADE_GLOW_TUNE` in `src/core/fx/bladeGlowMath.ts`, mirrored by a node test. A pixel brighter than the frame's lights could make any surface (`litCeiling`, from `World` and `Effects.litIrradianceNear` and the torch) is taken for glow and its hue is not used. It cuts the light at walls with an 8-tap march in screen space toward the blade (`BLADE_GLOW_MARCH`): a sample counts as blocked only when the depth is a little in front of it, not more than a thickness in front, and not nearer the eye than the path's nearer end by more than 0.3 m, which is what keeps a body between the camera and the path from shadowing the ground; one march per pixel, the player's blades together toward the strongest, or the strongest fighter's. While the pass is switched on the blades ask for no pooled flash lights (`App.bladeGlowOwnsLight`, which reads the setting, not whether a blade is on screen, so a blade crossing the screen's edge never swaps one light for the other). The pool serves the latest request when full, and flashes age only in `effects.update`, so what must keep a light is asked for last: the ship's room lights come after `world.update`, fighters' glows just before them. Blade light is read from the renderers (`SaberBlade.glowing`, `drawnBase`, `drawnTip`, `ignition`), not `saberSegments`, which skips the thrown and orbiting blades. A fighter's blade ends live on the fighter; they were module-level and every fighter's glow sat at the last one's blade. The frame context starts with `UNSET_BLADES` and the pass warns once if the game never hands its own list over.
- Three's renderer counts a frame per `render()` call, and the portal renderer makes several a frame: `scene.updateMatrixWorld()` runs once per call that takes the scene as its root (up to eight outside a building, three inside), and `Skeleton.update()` once per call for every skinned mesh drawn, re-uploading its bone texture each time. So `PortalRenderer.render` turns `scene.matrixWorldAutoUpdate` off after the first pass that walks the scene and puts it back in a `finally` (`matrixOnce`; `__debug.mobileTune({ passMatrices: 'every' })` measures without it). Nothing may move an object's transform between the passes of one frame; `onBeforeRender` may rewrite vertices (the engine trails do), never a matrix. Only not drawing a thing saves its skeleton, which is why a mobile is culled as a whole group.
- A converted actor is culled as one group against a sphere of its own (`plan.cull`, in its frame above its feet), by setting `group.visible`, and each of its meshes is then culled by three in every pass and shadow cascade against that same sphere, grown by `SKELETON_TUNE.sphereScale` and set once in the mesh's own frame at `attach` (`fitCullSpheres` in `src/world/bodyCull.ts`; a fighter's from its measured height): `Frustum.intersectsObject` reads a mesh's `boundingSphere` in that mesh's own frame and a skinned mesh computes one from its posed vertices only while it is null, so one set by hand is kept. The sphere is a standing body's, so anything that lays a body along the ground turns `frustumCulled` off for as long as it lies there (`cullsOneByOne`): a ragdoll, a body dead in its held death clip (every death on a world a server holds, and every one waiting in the ragdoll queue), a knockdown, a fighter prone, and an idle lying at full length (`LYING_IDLE`: the dead and restrained moods), each of which puts the head outside the sphere; every change of those asks again (`applyCull`). The manager owns `visible`, `castShadow` (at 2 Hz, by size class and screen, and never for a body whose sphere reaches none of the shadow cascades' light boxes, `reachesCascades` against the frusta three itself culls each cascade's casters by; never a distance from the camera, since the cascades' reach is a depth along the view and their boxes reach past it toward the sun) and `frustumCulled` on every mesh from `attach` on, whatever the prototype's preparation set. A clone shares one skeleton between the meshes that shared one in the model (`cloneShared`), and inside the portal renderer's passes a skeleton is worked out and uploaded once a frame (`src/core/skeletonOnce.ts`), which is one more reason nothing may move a bone between the passes of one frame.
- Turning shadows off must flip only `renderer.shadowMap.enabled` and leave the cascade lights' `castShadow` alone: with no shadow-casting directional light, three's cascade shader applies all three cascade lights unshadowed, three times the sun. With `castShadow` kept, it takes its "one light" branch.
- Ambient occlusion (`src/core/fx/ssao.ts`) darkens only the ambient share of each pixel's light. It re-evaluates, at the reconstructed surface, the lights that drew it, from `ctx.lights` (`src/core/fx/lights.ts`): `World.fillFxLights` gives the sky's hemisphere, fill and cascaded sun (one shadow tap) and the rooms' ambient, parallel light and lamps (each lamp with the cell `updateInteriorLights` lit it from), and `drawFrame` adds the flash pool and the torch. A light added to the game but not there makes AO too strong where it shines. Which set a pixel takes comes from the stencil the portal renderer leaves: inside a building 1 is rooms and 2 the world through exits; outside 1 is the world and 3 is rooms seen through doors (the door restore increments 2 to 3 rather than writing 1). It is read by an Equal-tested draw into a target sharing the scene's depth-stencil texture. Anything brighter than a surface lit that way could be is left alone, which keeps blades, bolts and glows from darkening, and water is skipped wherever the water mask's coverage is set. `__debug.ssao({ split: 0.5 })` compares, and `{ view: 'region' }` shows the rooms' pixels.
- A `sampler2DShadow` uniform must never be left null: three's empty shadow texture is never uploaded (its version stays 0), so null binds no texture, and ANGLE drops the whole draw with INVALID_OPERATION, silently once its error cap is hit. Bind a real 1x1 `DepthTexture` with a `compareFunction` and `needsUpdate = true` instead (the ambient occlusion's `noShadow`). That was three 0.185: three 0.187 uploads its own empty shadow texture with a compare that always passes, so a null there no longer drops the draw, and the game's stand-ins stay all the same. The half-resolution depth samples half texel i at full texel 2i + 1 (its centre at 2i + 1.5), so everything upsampling from it places a half texel there.
- The heat haze is the client's 2d_heat_composite: sources draw into a half-resolution RGBA16F buffer as (r, noise × r, noise × r) with additive blending, so overlaps average; the composite moves the UV by (noise − 0.5) × r × 0.01 × strength only where the landing pixel is hot, and not onto or from anything nearer than the farthest depth in the pixel's bilinear footprint. Sources sit in `HeatSources` (lava from World, plumes from providers that push numbers and a phase they integrate themselves; one provider that throws loses only its own plumes) and draw on layer 30 in the heat product's own scene, depth-tested by texel against `linearDepthHalf`. `gather` runs only inside `PostFX.end`; console helpers read stats. The client's noise volume has mean 0.49 and spread 0.077; the runtime 32³ noise matches it.
- The lens flare follows what the sky draws, not the light: a flare body is a `sun_*.sht` with a glow whose two sprites loaded (Yavin 4's sun slot is the gas giant and has none, Dathomir draws no sun, Mustafar's moon slot is a second sun and flares at night, in slot 1), and in space the brightest star sprite group, which is mostly nowhere near the zone's main light; each keeps a fixed slot, and `SwgSky.flareLights` reads the sprites' own position, opacity and `visible`. The sky's sprites hang at 3500 m and are depth-tested, so the occlusion counts depth as sky from 98% of that distance along each pixel's view axis, compared as `1 - depth` (exact near the far plane, unlike a linearised distance); fog does not dim the sprites, so it does not dim the flare. The occlusion is smoothed in a 2x1 target the element draw reads in its vertex shader, so nothing is read back. The composite never lifts a pixel past exposed luminance 0.9 (display 0.874, under the 0.888 where white begins) and adds nothing above it, which is why by day the flare is mostly a streak and ghosts; `__debug.flareProbe()` counts it, and `__debug.flare().sources[i].turn` is how to steer to a star when flying.
- Inside a building the world seen through its doorways is drawn after the rooms (the exits' stencil, a depth reset, the world pass), so anything additive drawn only with the rooms (`INTERIOR_LAYER`) is painted over wherever a doorway is behind it; outside, the world pass draws actors over the whole screen. The room motes (`RoomAir`) are on the actor layer, drawn in both passes and clipped by the stencil, and hidden on any frame the portal renderer draws from outside, since `cameraBuilding` can flip for a frame on the door line. The daylight beams are the doorways extruded along the sun, evaluated per pixel in the effects chain (`lightShafts`), where the scene depth stops them at whatever stands in them.
- A point sprite under about 3 px changes brightness up to eightfold as it crosses pixel boundaries (the Gaussian at 1.5 px; a hard disc twentyfold), which reads as twinkling: draw motes at 3.5 px or more with a Gaussian and no discard, and scale the colour by (true size / drawn size) squared.
- A third of a building's point lights sit outside their own cell's box, many in the ceiling above it, and exit polygons stick out of their cell's box by up to half a metre (Mustafar's crashed ship's torn bridge opening stands 3.8 m off its own plane): anything that clips to a cell box or casts a sight ray to a lamp must pad the box with the doorway's own vertices or clamp the lamp inside first.
- A light moved by setting its `position` keeps last frame's `matrixWorld` until the scene is drawn, and the effects' light records (`fillFxLights`) read the matrix: anything that reads the lights before the draw (the room's air does, because its motes are in the scene) sees last frame's places on the frame the room's lamps are handed out again, so `RoomAir` holds its lamps for that one frame and reads them all as new on the next.
- The aimed depth of field keeps its focus on the GPU in a 1×1 float ping-pong: the median of five half-resolution depth taps at the crosshair, eased in dioptres. The sky and anything nearer than the shooter's plane (`cam.orbitDistance + 0.4`, 0.6 m in first person) do not count. There is no lens until a surface has been under the crosshair this aim, and the last surface is held after. The aperture widens as the square root of the focus only to 40 m, since an unstopped aperture blurred the whole foreground whenever the focus went far. The lens-grid texels carry the raw circle of confusion, which rises with depth, so the gather orders taps without a depth fetch. The grid strides to a quarter on buffers taller than 2000 px, so the radius never outruns the ±3 tile dilation. Glows that write no depth (gun and ship particles, bursts, blade cores) are drawn into a `dofGlow` R16F product (1/z, MAX-blended, particles masked where bright), so a bolt at the focus does not take the blur of the town behind it. The game clears the aim on any frame the player is not simulated (dead, a panel, the map), or the shoulder view and the lens hold on under the death card. The wardrobe doll takes its lens pass (one render into a 4-sample half-float target, then a premultiplied gather, tone curve and sRGB onto its see-through canvas) only once zoomed in enough for a pixel of blur, with hysteresis to 0.7 px. Both program variants are compiled when the panel opens.
- **A light that must light rooms carries the actor layer, set once before anything compiles.** Three leaves a light out of a draw, and out of a compile, whenever its layers are not ones the camera sees, and the rooms pass sees only the interior and actor layers: the flashlight sat on layer 0 alone, so it lit the street through a doorway and no wall, floor or body inside any building, and the round of fixes that only moved and capped it changed nothing indoors. It is `markActor(this.torch)` straight after the torch joins the scene in `main.ts`'s constructor, so every warm-up builds the room programs with the spot already in their light set; both the draw camera (`PortalRenderer.renderLayer`) and the warm-up camera (`World.withLayers`) enable the actor layer, and changing a light's layers in play would rebuild every room program. Ambient occlusion re-evaluates the torch in `spotDirect()`, added after the region choice, so a room's pixels count it as well as the world's.
- **From inside a building the game draws only what its doorways show, and what moves is drawn only in the passes of the rooms it stands in.** The portal renderer used to draw the whole outdoor world, its shadows and its weather whenever any exit's sphere touched the view, every room of every building near enough, and every body and indoor chair in every pass, up to six times a frame with its bones uploaded each time. `portalVis.ts` floods the rooms outward from the camera's own room through each doorway's own outline on the screen, once a frame before any pass: only rooms really seen are drawn, with no exit in sight the world, the sun's shadows and the weather are not drawn at all, and when one is the world pass is cut to that exit's rectangle. It is conservative on purpose -- the projected polygon and never its plane (Mustafar's torn bridge opening stands 3.8 m off its own), padded rectangles, a camera standing in a doorway handing the room beyond the whole screen, every room whose box holds the camera starting the flood -- because every mistake it can make is a hole in a wall. `portalCull.ts`'s `ActorRoutes` then draws each body only in the pass of the room it stands in (outdoors: the world's and the shadows'), and also beyond any portal its sphere reaches, grown by how far it has gone since its room was last followed, so a body half through a doorway is drawn on both sides; a record with a light under it is never hidden, since a light hidden changes the light count and rebuilds every program; and a body left out of every view pass is kept hidden through the effects too, because the motion blur draws a mesh on its own only when it believes three drew it the frame before. A building's furniture is drawn with that building's rooms (`furnitureHost.ts`), and a body in a room nobody can see stops animating and casting. The Mos Eisley cantina went from 50 to 15 ms a frame and the street outside it from 59 to 24; every change is a switch `__debug.perf()` can flip back and forth on the same screen.
- **The stencil reference every material tests is one value read through an accessor on `THREE.Material.prototype`, never on a material.** Writing the portal renderer's reference into seven or eight thousand materials about nine times a frame cost 0.7 to 0.8 ms, so moving it is now one assignment (`PORTAL_REF.value`) and a registered material reads it through an accessor installed once, the first thing `main.ts` does, before any material exists (`src/world/stencilRef.ts`). On the prototype and only there: V8 turns an object whose data property is redefined as an accessor into a dictionary for good, and every field of that material then reads at about twice the cost (measured in node 22 on three's own materials: 60-80 ns for 25 reads in fast mode, 157-172 after an own accessor, and the same after a plain value is put back). The constructor's own `this.stencilRef = 0` goes through the setter, which writes two fields of the material's own in the same order for every material, so every material keeps one hidden class. A write from anywhere else is not taken, is counted and is said once in development, because a reference that silently would not move is the fault nobody would look for; `localStorage['swg.stencilAccessor'] = '0'` is the old way at the next load.
- **Three keeps an attribute's upload ranges until it uploads the attribute, and uploads it only when something draws it.** `addUpdateRange` pushes a new `{ start, count }` every call and only an upload empties the list, so a buffer refilled every frame and drawn only some frames -- the weather's own batches, which a room with no exit in sight never draws -- grew its list by an object a frame for as long as it went undrawn, and three sorted the whole pile the frame it was drawn again. A writer keeps one range of its own per attribute and hands it over once per upload, widening it in place while three has not taken it (`keepUploadRange`, `src/world/uploadRange.ts`); the marks keep their windows the same way. Anything that writes a buffer every frame and is not drawn every frame needs it.
- `Object3D.copy` deep-copies `userData` through JSON: never put a typed array or per-triangle data on a mesh's `userData` (the doll clones the character every time the wardrobe changes); per-triangle data belongs on the geometry's `userData` or in the owner's own structures.
- **The rooms' pool of lights is one shape a session, edited inside it by uniforms and places alone, and a room with no lights file is lit to the bit as before** (Map-maker II's wave of the rooms' lights: `src/world/roomLights.ts`'s `planRoomLights` and `ROOM_LIGHT_TUNE`, `roomLightPool.ts`, `roomLightEdits.ts`, `lightFlicker.ts`, `src/world/editFiles/lightsFile.ts`, `src/story/lightSpec.ts`, `src/editor/editorLights.ts`; `A:\swg3js\mapmaker2\LIGHTS-notes.md`). **Three keys every lit program on how many point, spot and directional lights a pass has and how many of each cast**, so the pool's shape -- the eight plain points it always had, the casting points, the spot slots plain and casting -- is the Graphics page's two settings (Room lamps, Room light shadows, both 0) and changes only by `RoomLightPool.setShape` behind a curtain (`App.lightPoolCurtain`, the effects warmed under it; out of play at once, and during a travel queued behind that journey's own screen, `roomPoolWait`, since a second curtain over a travel's screen hid the screen and dropped its programs); nothing else adds a light, takes one away or flips a `castShadow`. **A room's parallel light never casts**: three's cascades light a casting directional of index i only in cascade i's band of depth (CSMShader's `lights_fragment_begin`), so the first cut's casting twin of the parallel lit the near cascade's sixteen metres and nothing beyond;
