# Lessons: The player: rig, animation and combat

Moods, three's mixer, sabers and blaster aim, number slots, species rigs, dances, additive clips, first-person head hiding, where a blow came from.

Moved here from CLAUDE.md's "Things that took a long time to learn" on 2026-11-19, word for word and in their old order. Add a new lesson about this area here, never to CLAUDE.md. A code comment that cites "CLAUDE.md" or "the lesson in CLAUDE.md" means one of these files: grep `docs/lessons/` for its words.

- A character's moods are the animation table's `mood` selector, and it reaches the idle and nothing else: on the body table all twenty playable species share, the selector sits under the standing loop's still branch, so the walk and the run have none of its forty values. SOE's final table (with SOE's final names list) adds one mood under the walk and the run as well, `undead`, which the species packs carry as `idle:undead`, `walk:undead` and `run:undead` and nothing wears yet (the people's business, not a menu's). Of its 40 values 10 pick the very animation the default branch plays (`conversation` and `threaten` are the plain breathing loop under another name, as are the scene NPCs' terminal, consoling, accusing and imperial poses) and six more share a branch or a file with an earlier value, so the 40 come to 24 clips, about 5 MB of a 218 MB pack. They are baked as `idle:<label>` with every value that reaches one in the manifest's `variants`, which `rig.variant('idle', mood)` resolves; a mood with no branch falls back to `idle` and the body is unchanged, which is what a chat-only mood means. The shipped flattener cannot read them: it spreads each selector's variable and values over what it has already built as it unwinds, so the outermost wins and every mood branch comes out carrying the *gender's* values and no mood value at all — `tools/swg/moods.mjs` reads the table with the path-keeping flattener the mobiles command wrote, which is also why the two paths' spellings of an animation are compared normalised (the flatteners lowercase and strip a leading slash differently, and one uppercase letter would empty the join and convert nothing). That none of the forty touches the walk is read off the table and not off a failed lookup. Three of the 24 are single poses, so the player and parts bake pads every one-frame clip to two keys a frame apart as the mobiles packs always have: a zero-length action finishes on its first update and a repeating one poses at NaN. The chat's moods are the other table's 187 names, of which exactly 7 are in both, so the body takes a branch when there is one and the words take the mood always; the performances and the scene NPCs' death and sitting poses the same conversion writes can never be worn by a walking player.
- Three disables an AnimationAction when its fade-out finishes (`enabled = false`); an action reused after that shows its bones in the bind pose until `reset()` and `play()`. `rig.setState` restarts a legs action that has gone dead, and a saber move chained onto one already on the upper body stays there (`play(..., { upperOnly })`) rather than taking the whole body and fading the legs out; `__debug.split('BOTH_RUN2')` shows the halves and the live weights.
- Three's mixer writes a joint only when its clip's value moved since the last frame (`PropertyMixer` keeps the last value), so a joint something else turned after the mixer last frame still carries that turn on a frame the clip held it still. The head look turns the neck and head after the mixer and puts their clean pose back on its next call, but the first-person eye reads the head before that call, so on every frame the idle held the neck the eye stood where last frame's look had put it and the view shook back and forth by a centimetre. The rig now puts the look's joints back on their clean pose straight after `mixer.update` (`Rig.update`, `cleanLookBone`), and only a joint still holding last frame's turn, so a clip that wrote it this frame keeps what it wrote. Anything else that turns a bone after the mixer and is read before it next runs has the same trap.
- A lightsaber's appearance is an `.lsb` (form LSAT): BASE is the hilt's `.apt`, ASND its idle sound, a BLAD form holds SHDR (the blade's shader), LGTH and WDTH (metres), OPEN and CLOS (seconds), and an optional LGHT form the light (COLR as r, g, b, a bytes, RANG and TIME two floats each). The crafted sabers are under `object/weapon/melee/{sword,2h_sword,polearm}/crafted_saber/`; there is no `object/weapon/lightsaber/`. The blade colours are `palette/wp_lightsaber.pal`.
- Jedi Academy's torso does not face the view while moving: CG_PlayerAngles puts the legs at the movement offset (45 degrees for a strafe, 22 for a diagonal) and the torso at a quarter of it, so the shoulders lean a little the way the legs go. A torso turned all the way back to the camera read as twisted the wrong way. The special moves (katas, kicks, lunges, the jump attacks) are whole-body clips and must never be split off the legs; only plain swings ride the upper body over the run.
- The game's blaster poses point close to the view once the barrel is read along the true long axis. `correctAim` measures the barrel against the crosshair each frame and turns the spine up to `AIM_SPINE_MAX`, the body's heading beyond that; the tuned turns (`gunTune`) only apply with the correction off. Prone is left to the game's poses.
- The number slots are the same machinery for both classes: a list of defs with id, name, cost, kind and blurb (`forcePowers.ts` for the Jedi, `gadgets.ts` for the Bounty Hunter), a loadout of ids per kit (`setLoadout`), the Skills tab (`forceUi.ts`, given `defs` for the class in play) and a field per class on the saved character (`powers`, `gadgets`). Bare hands is a toggle in both lists: `player.fists` hides what is held, and `Unarmed` in `unarmed.ts` plays the game's `unarmed_standing_ready_*` clips (or `BOTH_MELEE1/2` and the kicks) with a capsule sweep part way in. The grenade templates are the weapons pack's `thrown` class, listed on the rack but not held; a gadget names its model by substring.
- A species' `rig.glb` is animations only and about 200 MB (1228 clips): `Character.load` parses it once per URL and shares the clips (`rigClips`), or every fighter spawned stalled the frame for seconds. A wearable from one skeleton on another (the human wardrobe on a Rodian) must have its `skinIndex` re-pointed by joint name (`remapSkin`), since three computes a skinned mesh's bounding sphere from its bones every frame and an undefined bone throws. The species index records each species' wardrobe folder (`wardrobe`); `catalogue()` reads it rather than probing folders. New shader programs are main-thread work even with `compileAsync`: `World.compileReady` does a mesh at a time with a yield between, and a fighter stays hidden until then.
- The dances are the skill loop's third branch (`loop_skill:speed2:dance_N`, N the performance table's visual id, odd novice and even full) and the flourishes `skill_action_1..8:dance_N`; `nameLocomotion` must not re-rank loop_skill's branches by speed. Emotes are the 270 `emt_` clips; the `_ag` ones are NPC ambient idles.
- `AnimationUtils.makeClipAdditive` rewrites a clip's values in place and leaves `clip.blendMode` behind as the mark. A clip shared between rigs (the species packs have been shared since the fighters stopped parsing them each) must only be converted once, and on the rig's own path, where the clip object itself is converted, `blendMode` is the whole guard and no marker of ours is needed. It is not a guard for a caller that wraps a clip: a wrapper built from `clip.tracks.slice()` shares the track objects, so the conversion lands on the shared values while the mark lands on the throwaway clip.
- Three skips an object with `visible = false` before the shadow pass looks at it, so hiding something that way removes its shadow too. What first person hides of the player goes on `FX_LAYERS.shadowOnly` instead: the portal renderer's shadow camera enables every layer and its view passes only 0 or 1 plus 31, so the shadow maps draw it and no view does. Layers are not in a program's key; nothing compiles on the switch, because `World.passesOf` treats the layer as an actor's, or a warm-up made in first person (an Effects switch, an arrival) builds the head's world-pass programs only and zooming out indoors compiles on the frame. `markActor` leaves a shadow-only mesh as it is, so nothing that prepares or marks the player un-hides the head; both it and `passesOf` ask `isShadowOnly` in `fxRegistry.ts`, which the node test calls. A draw with `FX_ALL_LAYERS` (the debug mask) sees shadow-only meshes too.
- What is "on the head" (`headRule` in `src/player/headHide.ts`) is decided per worn item from the client's data, never from mesh names: hair and the `hair/`, `wearables/hat|helmet|goggles/` template folders go whole; an item whose triangles all lie in head zones (skull, face, sideburns, neck) goes whole; a garment (it hides a zone below the neck) only ever loses its head triangles; an item hiding only head zones goes whole; otherwise the skin decides. A triangle is on the head when any vertex is weighted at least half to `Head` or below. Zones alone fail (some helmets have none, the Kashyyykian chest plates hide `skull`, the Mon Calamari body hides `skull`) and the hooded robe's arrangement does not take the `hat` slot; the skin is what tells the hood from the robe. Skin alone fails too: the Mandalorian Imperial helmet is skinned to `spine3` and only its zones hide it.
- A partly hidden mesh has its index reordered once so its head triangles are last, and its `onBeforeRender` narrows the geometry's draw range for the eye's passes while `onAfterRender` puts `Infinity` back; the shadow pass calls `onBeforeShadow` and draws it all. The range lives on the geometry only for the length of one draw, because the wardrobe doll's clone shares the geometry (and `clone()` never copies the hooks). The per-triangle zones are reordered with the index, and `Character.cull` culls through `cullIndex` (headHide.ts), which keeps the order and counts the kept triangles ahead of the head, so the kept head triangles stay last and the eye's count stays right.
- Where a blow on the player came from reaches the game by two different paths, and both have to carry it. Everything that strikes calls `damage(amount, from, push, source)` on the target, and `PlayerTarget` hands that on to the callback the frame loop sets; but a creature that is aggressive and *unprovoked* does not call `damage` at all — `Creature.update` calls the `onAttack` it was handed, which is `World.hurtPlayer`, which calls `playerTarget.hurt` directly. Anything reading the direction must be fed from both, or the commonest bite on a planet arrives with no side to it. So `onAttack` carries `from` the whole way down (`Creature.update`, `CreatureManager.update`, `World.update`, `World.hurtPlayer`) and `PlayerTarget.damage` hands it to its callback. `App` keeps one wrapper round `world.playerTarget.damage` as a second source, installed in its constructor — the record is one object for the life of the session — held for the length of the call in a `try`/`finally` so a direction cannot be left standing to be worn by the next blow that has none, and read by the frame loop’s damage closure as `from ?? this.hurtSource`. Every one of the nine places in `main.ts` that hurt the player goes through `App.hurtFrom`, which raises the red flash and, with a direction and within `HUD_WIRING.hurtRange`, the arc on that side; the direction is measured from `player.worldPos`, not `player.pos`, because aboard a ship's rooms `pos` is the hull's frame.
