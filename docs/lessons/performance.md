- **A loading screen stands what it brings you to only once the pack is in, waits a frame only where the frame shows something, and a travel to a place is one screen placed from the far world's own places file** (the performance pass's first wave: `src/world/loadingTune.ts`, `App.settle`, `App.teleport`, `App.standOnCourse`, `World.courseNeed`). `World.load` empties the people's rows (`standingPeople.unload`) and only `loadPack` hands them the new world's, so the two forced passes the arrival's warm-up made in between stood nobody on every path, and every town's people were stood on live frames after the screen lifted (27 to 29 colour programs in the first 30 s, 360 to 520 ms frames on Direct3D 11; `standOrder.test.ts` drives the real `StandingPeople` in that order). The settle stands them instead: the people after its first settled loop (the catalogue awaited, bounded, since a first world finds it still in flight), the ticket collectors there too (`standCollectorsNow`, which `standTravelRest` must not push twice), the people of ours and the creatures after the shuttles (`standOursNow`, `standWildNow`, a wild pass forced past its hold and its few sites a pass, a site still waiting for its ground stepped over and tried again inside the bound, which the wait holds for even with every body up, since a site that waited has stood nobody to keep it), the people again just before the sweep, and then their bodies waited for up to `standWaitMs` from the first stand; a body still loading at the bound is left to its load and counted (`afterBound`). The sweep waited a whole frame per batch of eight whatever it built -- 28 s of a 44 s screen at Mos Eisley, its own work 0.45 s -- so behind a screen it waits only after a batch that built a program or after `sliceMs` of work (`sweepYield`, `World.sweepBatches`); in play, the Effects switch's sweep, every batch still waits, and a hidden tab's message-channel frame is untouched. Each batch that built is timed into the session's milliseconds a program (`programMs`), which is what the screen's words and the pacing's `hard` read: the trivial probe timed straight after the game's own draws timed those draws (267 ms for a link that costs half a millisecond), so it decides only whether the Interface page blames the driver, three links on frames of their own after the sweep, each after a drain (`checkDriver`), the least kept. The world is drawn at most every `drawEveryMs` while the screen is fully opaque (`LoadingScreen.opaque`, past its half-second fade and before `hide`), every frame from the reveal on. A place on another world is worked out in the game's frame from that world's `pois.json` centre before anybody travels (`placeFromPois`, teleport's own two lines, which `loadingRules.test.ts` runs as text), and the travel comes out there with `ground` and `port`: stood on the real terrain, kept as the spawn and stepped into a port's building **before** `cameOut` and the saved place, or the group's word and the saved place are the world's spawn (the followers came out at Bestine); a follower's `besideAt` still wins, and no centre, space, or a layout centre that disagrees (`centreAgrees`, the safety net) runs the two old phases exactly as they were. A race start asks a strict need test before anything is shown (`raceCurtainNeed` over `World.courseNeed`: the hull within `courseNear`, the grid settled, every model `LayoutStreamer.modelsAlong` finds along the legs sampled every `courseStep` loaded and warmed, nothing queued), because a start with no curtain can load and warm nothing (no screen is up for the warm to load under); otherwise a course curtain that never calls `settle()`: the grid's ground waited for only when the hull was far, the course's models loaded for at most `TRACK_TUNE.warmWaitMs` (`loadBounded`, which races the loads against a timer, so a load that never settles cannot hold the curtain) and compiled through stand-ins (`compileCourse`, which must never call `placedStandIns`, since that consumes the placed warm's list), the queue flushed, the screen hidden at once. A model known not to load (`LayoutStreamer.hasFailed`, noted by `loadModel` as by a tier) is counted apart (`courseCounts`' `failed`) and never asked for again, or one broken file along a course took a curtain on every start; and the session's memo of what a course warmed is let go with the streamer at `unload`, or it held the world left behind's whole layout. It keeps `traveling` for the seventy-odd places that read it and sets `curtainOnly` beside it, which only the story's leave, a call's end and the world editor's own let-go read (the editor stays on through a curtain of its own, Map-maker II's third wave): a start is a moment at the grid, not a journey, and must not end a call or say the player left every area. And the queue's idle rule judges frames counted, never the wall time since the last tick: a frame that built a program for 1.6 s read as a stopped loop to the first timer after it, which flushed the rest of the queue in that timer task, outside any frame (37 of 170 programs of a Bestine session); a waiter now needs two of its own polls spanning more than `idleMs` with no new tick (`QueueWatch`), the single-instant entry test is a 2 s backstop, **both take `idleShownMs` (10 s) on a page on screen** -- there the browser holds the frames back while its GPU process catches up, with the main thread free and the waiters' timers running (486 ms with no frame after a cold Direct3D 11 Mos Eisley screen lifted, read as a stop at 250 ms, and the flush that followed blocked the page 3.9 s), so only a hidden tab, whose frames really stop, is answered quickly -- the frame loop ticks the queue again after its draw, and a flush in play takes only the jobs there when it began and stops the moment frames resume. `flushedInPlay` counts every program the idle rule builds on a page on screen with no loading screen up, a waiter's flush (`flushPlay.shown`) or the entry test's own outright build (`noteIdleBuilt`), which must stay nought: counted only when it built within `idleMs` of a frame's tick, it could never rise, since the flush begins only after its waiter saw no tick for longer than that and stops at the first one. `programPace.test.ts`'s day-one half loads the old queue out of git at `5f3cff4` and shows it calling that 1.6 s frame a stop. A queued compile that ends on a room's pass puts the world pass's lights back with a compile of nothing under layer 0 (`compileOne`), or the next shadow pass keyed three's depth material on the room set; outdoors beside buildings a frame's last real pass is still a room's, so skinned depth programs are still built in play there until per-pass render states (wave 2). Four things the smoke taught. **The driver check times a link only while the world builds nothing** (`World.compileBusy`: no outright build, nothing queued, no flush; never "every body up", which a town whose wildlife was still landing never was), and one screen a session, the first whose check is not done, waits up to `driverWaitMs` for its tries ("checking the graphics driver"), every later one letting the check run on over its frames: a body landing behind a screen builds outright with a frame's breath per object, so the hundred milliseconds a screen used to have after its sweep were all busy, and on Vulkan the check never ran over two arrivals (`shaders().driver.waits` says why it is still waiting); held for it on every screen until done, a cold Direct3D 11 town whose bodies were still landing as each screen lifted paid a second a screen for a sentence on the Interface page. On headless Direct3D 11 a trivial link really costs about 235 ms with its drain at 0.3 ms, measured on a page of its own, so the check calling that machine slow is the check working; Vulkan's is 0.5 ms. **A course curtain warms shadow depth only when something on the course sways** and draws no frame of its own: both together were most of a 3.2 to 3.8 s warm curtain, which is 2.1 s without them. **What was built for a body up as the screen lifted is read off three's own records of that body's materials** (`after.forUp`: `World.readyBodyMaterials` kept as the screen lifts, then `programsForUp` over `renderer.properties.get(material).programs` for the ids past the renderer's highest at the lift and inside the window), never by whether a program is skinned, since a body stood after the screen builds skinned programs too, and **never by the queue label of the body's prepare**: a body is up only once its prepare has built, so a label-keyed count could see nothing but a prepare made afresh, while the programs the target is about (a room set, a recolour, three's own first draw) are built under other labels or outside the queue -- it read nought in every session whatever happened. The queue's labels (`model:`, `look:`, `weapon:`, `hologram:`) still name what `lastBuilt` shows. **A counter that proves a target must be able to rise in the game**: before trusting a nought, find the one case the game can reach that makes it one. And **two sessions' program keys are compared by key, never by name or a hash of three's key text for a shader material**: `shaders({ full: true })` names a key after the first material that ever built it, and a `ShaderMaterial`'s key carries three's shader-cache ids, numbered in the order a session first met each source, so the same effect pass hashes differently in two sessions; what remained after setting those apart, between a run with `sliceMs` 0 and one with the default, was the crowd's own random draw of who stands where.
- **Three keys a program on the light state of the scene object it is drawn under, so every pass draws through a scene of its own, and a program built in play is issued and polled, never waited for** (the performance pass's second wave: `src/world/passScenes.ts`, `src/core/linkMode.ts`, `src/world/roomDefer.ts`, `src/core/skinnedLoad.ts`, `src/world/personKeys.ts`, the motion blur's own jobs in `src/core/fx/velocity.ts`, `src/player/bytesHash.ts`, `src/player/partsFile.ts`). The shadow pass and the rooms pass now draw and compile through proxy scenes that share the real scene's `children` array by reference (never a copy, never a reparent) and copy its fog, environment, background and override material at each pass (`SCENE_FIELDS`), each keeping a light state of its own; they copy `matrixWorldAutoUpdate` too, so the portal renderer's one matrix walk a frame still holds. Two things were learned getting T9 to nought. **An object a flight saw ready finishes only what reports its link done** (`resolveLinks`' `onlyReady`): its materials may be shared, and another job may have issued a program for them in another pass since (a second person of one look whose first person's rooms pass is in flight), and finished there that link was waited for on the main thread, 386 ms on Direct3D 11. And **a program `compileAsync` warmed is not finished**: it waits for the link and never asks for the uniforms, so its first draw asks, synchronously, and in play that question waits behind whatever the GPU process is linking for somebody else (two water-mask programs the effects warmed behind the first screen, first drawn 84 s later, waited 111 ms each); `PostFX.warmUp` resolves what it warmed before it returns. The link-wait watch (`watchLinkWaits`) wraps every GL call on the game's context that can wait on a link, timing it whoever calls (three's own first draw included) and leaving the completion polls untimed, and T9 is read there, never off the queue's own clock. **ANGLE on Direct3D 11 builds a second vertex shader on the GPU process the first time a skinned program draws when the joints are unsigned shorts** (25 to 57 ms of the whole browser stalled), so every skinned mesh's joints are stored as floats at load (`skinnedPlugin`, registered by `surfaces.withPlugin` and beside it at every other loader of a skinned model, a text test holding every `new GLTFLoader(` to it, the rig's animation-only loader excepted); and every skinned primitive's morph count is padded with zero targets to the next rung of `PERSON_KEYS_TUNE.morphLadder` (0, 7, 33), so the motion blur's warm variants (8) and the skinned depth combinations (27) are closed lists of ours, which `warmLists.test.ts` holds every converted primitive inside. Three has built each mesh's `morphTargetInfluences` by `afterRoot`, so the padding extends every built mesh's influences in the same step, never through `updateMorphTargets` (which renames the targets the body sliders read): padded in the geometry alone, `getVertexPosition` and a skinned mesh's bounding sphere read an undefined influence and come out NaN. The two cost 3.1% of the same people's model memory (264.0 against 272.3 MB at Bestine with the budget out of the way), so `MOBILE_CACHE.budget` went from 180 to 190 MB; `swg.floatJoints` and `swg.morphLadder` at '0' are the hatches. **A motion-blur variant is keyed on the empty light state of its own draws**, so one met in play goes on the world's queue as a job with its own compile (its dummy as its own scene under the effects' target) and finish (`OwnJob`), never through `compileOne`, which would key it on a pass's lights and leave its first real draw to build it again; a job let go unbuilt says so (`dropped`) and the next draw asks again. **A body stood outdoors is shown once its world pass is built, and its rooms pass follows as work of its own** (`compileReady`'s `roomsLater`): it is never shown in a building pass until that answers (`ActorRoutes.add`'s `roomReady`, a wide record flagged `F_NOROOM` rather than skipped, a lit one left drawn and counted), with the routes off its root is hidden round each rooms pass (`roomPending`), a body in a room while not ready has its job made urgent (`roomsNow`), and a body with a light under it is never deferred. **A shared look's renders are made in the characters' worker with the pixels' hash made there** (`Customizer.shareRender`, `hash: true`): over a two-minute Vulkan walk the main thread's look renders were 62 stretches of 40 ms or more between frames, 5.2 s, and are none; a customizer that renders elsewhere refuses a share outright, since `run` takes its private branch first. And a parts pack's `parts.json` is fetched and parsed once a folder for the session (`partsFile`), out of `character.ts` because a parameter property keeps that file from node. Four things the review found, each of which every behaviour test passed. **Work deferred off the frame is tied to the life of what it compiles, both ways round** (`deferRooms`): a cached look trimmed while its rooms batch waited had that batch compile its disposed materials (programs nothing releases, and the cascades' strong map taking them back), so the owner going marks the handle `gone` and the work compiles nothing; and a world's unload clears the queue, answering every batch unbuilt: a handle marked ready on that answer put a body of the cached look in a rooms pass with no program in the next world, so work let go (`OwnJob.dropped`) leaves the handle not ready and asks again (`promote` urgent, `resume` below it). **A check made while the tab is hidden decides nothing** (`LinkCheck` waits a hidden spell out and starts its window again when the tab shows, polling the program it already compiled): decided on, a page that entered play hidden was `blocking` for its whole session. **A program three destroyed in flight has no handle** (`program` undefined; `isReady` then answers null for ever), so the flight strikes it off (`programGone`) rather than waiting the bound and blaming the driver. - **The scene's one matrix walk a frame steps over a body nothing drew and a body nothing posed, so every write under a creature's or person's root says so** (the performance pass's third wave: `src/world/mobiles/bodyGroup.ts`, `crowd.ts`, the tiers' last part in `lod.ts`, `Mobile.posed`). - **The sun's middle and far cascades are drawn by age and held where their maps were drawn, and a held light is put back between `csm.update()` and `refreshCascadeBoxes`, never after** (the performance pass's sixth wave, built fourth: `src/world/shadowCadence.ts`, `shadowBatches.ts`, `shadowDials.ts`, the hooks in `portalRender.ts`).
