# Lessons: Materials and effects

Normal maps, detail maps, the mirror mask, the client's highlight, model reflections, unread slots, particles and those carried in a hull's frame, ribbons, flip-books, scrolling, effect passes, emissive split, animated surfaces.

Moved here from CLAUDE.md's "Things that took a long time to learn" on 2026-11-19, word for word and in their old order. Add a new lesson about this area here, never to CLAUDE.md. A code comment that cites "CLAUDE.md" or "the lesson in CLAUDE.md" means one of these files: grep `docs/lessons/` for its words.

- The converter writes normal maps exactly as the game has them and the game reads the green as it stands, `normalScale (1, 1)`. It was `(1, -1)` for years, on the reasoning that the game's maps are Direct3D's (green down) and the glTF loader flips a map without tangents; by eye on a wall's edges that is the wrong way up, and the owner's call is that it is not the flip that is wrong but the whole chain of reasoning about where the flip happens. `__debug.normals(1, -1)` puts the old way back live to compare.
- **A surface is its colour texture times a detail texture, at a coordinate set of its own.** All twenty-six of the client's detail programs are the same line -- `result.rgb = diffuseColor * detailColor * light`, or with a specular map `diffuseColor * allDiffuseLight * detailColor + allSpecularLight` -- so it multiplies the **diffuse** term and never the specular, which is what multiplying the base colour does in three. It reads **coordinate set 1**: every detail vertex program writes `#define textureCoordinateSetDETA textureCoordinateSet1`, and the effect file says so in its own bytes (the first byte of each pass's PTXM chunk is the set index, 0 on MAIN, 1 on DETA, 2 on SPEC). That set is **not** the main one scaled -- measured over the 12,632 retail vertex arrays belonging to a detail shader, only 60.7% fit `u1 = a*u0 + b` and the rest miss by up to 137 texture units -- so it is carried (`msh.mjs` keeps `uvs2`, `glb.mjs` writes `TEXCOORD_1`, GLTFLoader names it `uv1`) and a per-material tiling factor would have been wrong on four surfaces in ten. 1,369 shaders over 281 images; Tatooine alone is 297 surfaces on 36% of its placed objects. Getting `uv1` into a three material is the awkward part: three decides `USE_UV1` in `getParameters`, **before** `onBeforeCompile` runs, so an injection cannot ask for it -- but `customDefines` is emitted at the top of the vertex prefix and the `#ifdef USE_UV1 / attribute vec2 uv1;` block a hundred and fifty lines below it, so `material.defines.USE_UV1 = ''` is enough and no decoy ao map is needed. The injection is `src/world/detailMap.ts`, shaped like `injectWetness` and wrapped in the same pass; a material naming a detail map with **no** second set under it is written nothing at all, because an unset attribute reads as zero and would sample one texel over the whole surface.
- **How much of a surface is a mirror is a second mask, and it is not always in the colour texture's alpha.** The client's line is `result.rgb = lerp(diffuseLitSurface, envColor, envMask) + allSpecularLight`, so `envMask` is metalness in three's terms with the scene's environment behind it. Measured over the 1,400 retail shaders with a reflection cube (before SOE's final names list, which brings 1,407): **981 keep the mask in MAIN's alpha, 378 in the MASK slot's alpha, 25 in SPEC's, and 16 cannot be read**. On the whole two-tone customizable family (`h_color2_envmask_*`) MAIN's alpha is the **hue** mask -- a shape mask with nothing to do with reflection -- so reading it there made an armour piece mirror-bright wherever its second colour fell, and on 600 more the *gloss map* was standing in for it, which makes a hull shiny instead of chrome. `envMaskOf` (`surface.mjs`) reads it off the pixel program the way `emissiveOf` reads the glow's, in the four shapes the archives use: the plain `envMask = tex2D(x, uv).a`, the one that reuses a local name (the hue programs declare `sample` twice), the assembly `lrp r0, t0.a, t1, r0` whose t1 is the cube, and that same lerp with the mask taken through a register (`mov r0, t0` first). Roughness and metalness are now two textures sampled into one image. And **IRID is a cube under another name**: the fourteen iridescent shaders (the chitin armour) name theirs IRID and are drawn by the very same envmask program with that slot on its `envMap` sampler, so every test for a reflection has to accept both names or that family is the one reflective thing in the game with no reflection at all.
- **A normal map is decoded by the slot it sits in, and a surface's shine is the mask its own program multiplies its specular by** (material format 6: `surfaceReaders`, `normalReadOf`, `specMaskOf` and `materialOf` in `tools/swg/surface.mjs`, the decoder in `src/swg/normalDecode.ts`). Over all 22,322 retail shaders (measured before SOE's final names list, which brings 22,902) every program that reads a CNRM slot decodes the compressed layout (x in the alpha, y in the green, z rebuilt) and every one that reads NRML plain signed RGB, so the slot alone decides; the converter guessed from the picture instead (an alpha that varied more than the red was taken for x), and an uncompressed map keeps the height it was made from in its alpha, so 1,157 used shaders -- 97 of Tatooine's 114 bumped ones -- were lit about 33 degrees off, and the recolour path (`recipeNormal`) repeated the guess over every recoloured worn piece until it too decoded by the tag it already had. The mask is the texture and channel the program names (SPEC's **alpha**, not its luminance; the normal map's alpha on the two-tone cloth, whose colour alpha is the hue mask; MASK's red where a float is set from `.rgb`), written into the metal-rough image's **red**, which three never reads; green stays the roughness from our own 0.85 slope because reflections and the rain's wet look read it; a mask of one everywhere writes no image. Five things cost time. A pixel program is an IFF and its text is the PSRC chunk, whose first line (`//hlsl`) is the only thing that says it is HLSL (`programSource`); read raw, the binary header hides it. In assembly the specular is `v1` and the specular constants followed through the registers, and a lookup (a `texm3x2tex` result, an LKUP table) is part of the specular and never its mask, or the aniso tables read as masks. A program that multiplies the mask in twice (`allSpecularLight * specularMask` after the raw term already has it) is `squared`, and a ps 1.1 one that applies it once where the specular is added is not. A shader whose program reads a SPEC slot it names no file for is drawn with a mask of one (what an unbound sampler gives) and logged. And the metal-rough image is named for its masks (`glbImages`), since two shaders on one colour texture whose programs read their shine from two places were one image in a GLB. What the highlight is goes on every textured material as `extras.swgSpec` (mode `phong`, `aniso` where the program calls the client's band function, or `none`; the MATL's fourth group as the colour and its seventeenth float as the power) and `extras.swgCube`, **never under `extras.swg`**: a transparent material with `userData.swg` is drawn after the water, and the surfaces plugin walks every material of a model once one carries `extras.swg`. A material with no cube has `metallicFactor` 0. A normal map the shader's TCSS puts on set 1 is drawn on `uv1` (on 139 of the 324 vertex arrays that draw such a shader set 1 is not set 0 scaled), with the plain copy for a primitive without the set; a mask or normal map on a set the models do not carry (NRML 2, MASK 2 and 3, SPEC 1) is drawn on the main's and counted, as is a program that cannot be read (the 17 used shaders with none keep the old rule). `player`, `parts` and `species` finally stamp `materialFormat` (and `creatures`, `space`, `sandbox`, `spawns` and `travel` with them), and `status` also reads the first textured model each of the three lists for `extras.swgSpec` out of its JSON chunk (`glbSpecStamped`, `glbsCurrent`): a stamp a later command copied forward must not pass for a new model. The mobiles carry the format in their models' and wearables' signatures (`m`) and not in `MOBILES_FORMAT`, which would rebake every animation pack for nothing; and since a catalogue planned before holds the old signatures, which its units' records still match, `status` reads the catalogue's own `materialFormat` to know a run would find them stale. Four more were found in review. **Every pass of the implementation is read, each through its own samplers** (`passState`'s `passes`, `firstPassThat`): a blended surface draws its colour in one pass and adds its highlight in a second, so read off the first pass alone 76 used shaders -- most of the long hair, the glass, the lenses, the bacta gels -- came out drawing no highlight at all, and one membrane lost its normal map; one light-pass program reads its mask from SPEC under one effect and from MASK under another, which is why the samplers are per pass (the mirror and the glow are never only in a later pass, measured, and stay the first pass's). **A skinned shader's normal map takes its slot from the plain path and its file from the run's own values** (`skinnedTexture`): the plain path describes a customizable shader by its base shader's files, not its choices, and the human male's face's base names the fourth wrinkle map while its default age chooses the first. **A command that keeps a model because its file is there must ask what the record it wrote last time stood for** (`materialStale`; travel's `travelModelsToConvert`, the spawns' `nestModels`), or the run a stamp asked for writes the stamp over the very models it was asked to redo and nothing asks again; the spawns stamp only from a run that made its models. And in `status` nothing may sit between an `if` and its `else if`: the snapshot's surface counts were tallied in an `if` of their own there and took the detail levels' `else` for themselves. Every test of it decodes pixels out of a GLB the converter's own readers wrote (`normals.test.ts`, `specMask.test.ts`, run on any pack folder through `SWG3JS_PACKS`). Measured after the pass's one conversion: every model a pack names was written again except five on Tatooine that the snapshot did not rewrite (the cantina's far level, Watto's junk shop and its two pieces, the Lucky Despot's engine debris and two house pieces, last written 2026-09-07). **What skipped them is the manifest, not a model**: the snapshot read the manifest it found and kept every category in it, so the old `pack` command's `structures`, `rocks`, `debris` and `vaporators` rode along on Tatooine under a `materialFormat` the run never made true of them, and those five (seven counting the far levels) are theirs; nothing with a layout draws them (the scatter loads rocks and debris and draws them only on a world with no flora). The snapshot now keeps only the categories it makes (`snapshotCategories` in `tools/swg/worldManifest.mjs`: `layout` and `flora`) and says which it dropped, `status` asks for the snapshot while a world's manifest carries any other, and `worldStamps.test.ts` holds every textured model a world's layout and flora name, and their lower levels, to `glbSpecStamped` (7,691 over the nineteen worlds once SOE's final files were converted, 7,656 before, every one stamped) and names a world still carrying a stray category, which on an install converted before the fix is Tatooine until its snapshot runs again; a pack folder also holds files no manifest names any more (793 in one wardrobe folder), which the tests step over by the stamp rather than read as a writer's fault.
- **A surface's highlight is the client's own line, put in place of three's GGX, and three's lights are worked out in functions that cannot see `main()`** (`src/world/specular.ts`, its arithmetic mirrored line for line in `src/world/specMath.ts`). The client drew the specular colour times the mask times (N·H) to the power, with no Fresnel term and no N·L factor; on its fake-anisotropic programs a band of N·H instead (one less the distance of 1.25 N·H from one, to the power of 0.15 p, less 0.15, never below nought), times N·L, which peaks at N·H 0.8 at 0.85 of the colour: a ring 37 degrees off the mirror direction, which no GGX roughness can make. Its highlight was measured against a white surface facing the light, which is the light over pi in three, so the term is divided by pi and a colour of 0.5 is half a sunlit white wall. The injection expands `#include <lights_physical_pars_fragment>` and replaces three 0.185's one line in `RE_Direct_Physical` (`irradiance * BRDF_GGX_Multiscatter(...)`, which three 0.187 took out and `src/core/threeHolds.ts` puts back before any program is built), or nothing at all if the line is not there. **The mask and the wet amounts are read in `main()` into globals**, because `RE_Direct_Physical` is defined above `main()` and three's `texelRoughness` (whose red is the client's mask, which three never reads) and the wet chunk's `wetSurface`/`wetPuddle` are `main()`'s locals; the wet chunk now says `#define SWG_WET` with its declarations so the highlight can ask. A wet or snowed surface keeps three's GGX by the largest of how wet it is, its puddle and the snow on it (`film`, `weatherShare`), since the rain's glint and the snow's matt both ride on roughness, which the client's term never reads: with the rain alone in it, every phong roof under settled snow kept a highlight brighter than the snow beside it. The mode, colour and power are per-material uniforms kept in a `WeakMap` so a recompile reuses them, and the knobs are shared by reference (`SPEC_UNIFORMS`), so every converted material shares one program text under the key prefix `spec`, put on last of the four in `World.adoptMaterials` (cascades, wet, detail, highlight) and only on a standard material carrying `userData.swgSpec`: a pack from before format 6 draws three's GGX exactly as before, and mode `none` (every leaf, every shader whose program has no specular) draws no highlight rather than three's. Every base is held above nought before `pow` (N·H below nought, a power of nought, a light behind the eye), since a NaN pixel is a black box once the bloom spreads it. Ours: the cap at a white surface facing the light (the client clamped every pixel to white and an HDR frame does not; glass's colour of 10 would bloom), `gate`, `film` and the live switch back to GGX (`__debug.spec({ use: 'three' })`, a uniform); `localStorage['swg.spec'] = 'three'` skips the wrap at load, because a program that fails to compile draws nothing. **A surface whose shader has no reflection cube takes none of the environment** (`userData.swgCube === false`: `envMapIntensity` times `REFLECTIONS.plain`, nought): in three 0.185 an environment map also lights the diffuse of the material that carries it, so a registered plain wall wore a faint mirror and a sky-coloured fill its matt neighbours lacked, neither of which the client drew. It stays registered with its map, so its program does not change and `__debug.reflections({ plain: 1 })` compares live; **a copy of a registered material is registered with its original's own strength** (`registerReflective(copy, original)`, the interior cells' copies), because once an environment is in force the original's field holds what the registry wrote into it, nought on a plain wall, so read off the copy every room's plain walls stayed at nought under `plain: 1` and every mirroring copy took the scale twice. **A preview wraps what the world has not adopted** (`specWrapTree` in `CharacterPreview.refresh`, `specWrap` in `ShipPreview`'s prepare): the select screen and the creator's dark stage never reach the world's scan, so their figure wore three's GGX on a fresh session and the client's once the rig had been played; the world builds nothing more for it, since the cascades replace the hook and the scan wraps it again under the same key. `__debug.specSelfTest()` draws a lit sphere through the injected program against `specMath.ts`, the switch to GGX against an unwrapped material (building no program), and the weather's film through a stand-in for the wet chunk, and builds four programs of its own, so it runs after `shaders().builtInPlay` is read, never before.
- **What is worn, held, flown and ridden, and the creatures, reflect the world's sky only where their shader has a cube, and they join the registry as the world adopts them, never as they load** (`joinModelReflections` in `src/world/envmap.ts`, called at the first sight of a material in `World.adoptMaterials`, which reaches everything else the world loads outside a planet's own pack as well: a door, a shuttle, a camp's tents). Nothing outside `AssetPack` registered before, so the worn-side shaders with a cube (wardrobe 150, mobiles 77, characters 9, creatures 5), 100 weapons' and 45 ships' drew their chrome dark. The environment is in three's program key, and adoption is the one step every path that loads something in play takes before it compiles (`prepareActor`, `prepareVehicle`, `prepareExtras`, `compileReady`, the streamer, the sweeps), so a model is built with its reflection once, behind whatever prepares it; `modelReflections.test.ts` reads every one of those paths and the hooks the game hands its loaders. A plain shader and a pack from before format 6 are left out (neither reflected before), and a planet's own material is never made a model's (its pack registered it). **A copy takes its original's strength wherever it is made** (a ship's paint, a clear pane), because `registerReflective` keeps the strength on the material's userData, which `clone()` and a spread both carry: read off the copy's own field it took whatever the registry had last written into the original, nought while the models were switched off. **The previews draw the same materials in WebGL contexts of their own, and swap a studio of their own onto them around each of their own draws and compiles** (`EnvironmentSwap`, `src/ui/previewStudio.ts`: the doll, the select screen, the creator, the ship edit page), putting the world's back in a `finally` and setting nothing that asks three for a program again: a texture the world's renderer filtered is nothing in another context, and a swap left standing across an await would hand the world's renderer the preview's. **A swap takes each material once, however many meshes wear it** (`take` steps over one already carrying its studio, which nothing but the swap ever puts on a material): a material the world never registered -- a figure on the select screen, a hull never spawned this session, a preview's own paint copies -- has no strength but its own field, which the first meeting writes, so taken at every meeting four guns cloned from one part file drew at a sixteenth of their share where the swap meant half, and the same armour previewed dimmer before it had been played than after. The doll makes its studio through `StudioChoice` (asked once a clone, made once). The studio is filtered from a 128 px cube like every environment the world hands out, so a program keyed with either is keyed alike. The ship edit page gives its room to a format-6 hull's plain parts at `plain` (nought), as the world gives them its sky, where it used to light every material with it as the scene's environment; an older pack's still takes it that way. **Every registered material carries a black stand-in while the world has none** (`setEnvironmentStandIn`, the water's own, handed over at boot): with none, a world going took the environment out of every registered program, which cost nothing for a planet's own materials, unloaded with it, and built a program on a live frame for anything carried across -- the hull a jump carries to another system, and its crew's armour, are drawn all through the unload, inside the tunnel. Black reflects and fills nothing, so it draws exactly what no environment drew, and the next sky is a texture swapped in. `__debug.reflections({ models: false })` writes the models at nought live (a uniform), and `localStorage['swg.reflections.models'] = '0'` keeps them out of the registry at load, which is exactly how they were drawn before.
- **Slots deliberately left unread, each with the measurement that decided it.** LKUP, 2,653 shaders: `a_specmap_bump_ps20.psh` declares `diffuseMap` and `normalMap` and nothing else -- LKUP is the fixed-function path's lighting table and the modern program never samples it. MNSM, 29: a half-resolution copy of MAIN, on sky bodies only. The `2blend_dirt` family (DIRT, DTLA, DTLB, 162 apiece): four textures on four coordinate sets with no MAIN in the program at all (`lerp(detailB, detailA, mask) * dirt`), reaching 1.0% to 4.9% of a world's placed objects, and already drawing a sensible single texture. DCAL 40, REP0 16, MFFP 25 (the water effect's second pass), and the half-dozen singletons. **A fountain's or a pool's basin water is drawn by the water system** (the owner's call, 2026-09-26): each outdoor copy's level water surface becomes a lake body (`World.basinWaterBody`, through `LayoutStreamer.waterSurface`), so it reflects through the water pass, ripples and takes rain rings, and never swells; the model's own piece is then not drawn for that copy. Which shaders count is an exact list (`BASIN_WATER_SHADERS` in `surfaces.ts`), each measured level over the whole of its surface: the Corellian, Theed and statue fountains, the garden fountains, the swimming pool and the Tusken fort's pool. Falling water, foam, geyser bowls, tanks and canisters stay as the model draws them, and a basin standing in a room stays the model's, since the room pass does not draw world water bodies. A basin wears a look of its own (`BASIN_WATER_TUNE` in `basinWater.ts`, a clear green-grey well under the planet's lake opacity: the lake colour read as a swimming pool in a stone bowl), is left out of the model's colliders (every triangle of a placed model is one, so the surface was a floor you stood on and nothing you did rippled it), and its footprint -- the level triangles flattened onto the ground, never the box, whose corners reach the paving round a round fountain -- answers `footSurfaces.waterTop`, the splashes' height and the ripple field's level near the player: the field is one plane, so beside a basin it takes the basin's. A fountain's own spray is drawn at `FOUNTAIN_SPRAY_TUNE.alpha` of its alpha (`particleDraw.ts`, every `pt_fountain_*` but the one brazier that shares the name): the files are right, a white texture averaging alpha 0.23, but a hundred-odd quads in the air stack to a sheet. `__debug.fountains` moves all of it.
- A particle effect placed with a `frame` (a hull's live matrixWorld) simulates in that frame and is carried into the world as its quads are built: anything held or played aboard is placed that way, and the planet's ground and the weather's kill are never applied to it. A held flame's heat is stamped each step and ignored when not held within 100 ms; `BountyHunterKit.coolDown()` (class switch, leaving a ship) drops the flame's heat and its held effect.
- A particle can carry other effects (a `PATT` in its `PTCL`: path, a start-percent range, kill-with-particle, and spawn on creation, death, a percent of life or ground contact). `exportParticle` converts them through its `attach` callback (`convertParticle` recurses, with a `{ pending: true }` placeholder in its cache against cycles) and writes each one's `file` onto the attachment. The game places them as transient effects in the parent's frame, one level deeper (at most 12), follows the particle each frame and, with kill-with-particle, sets their `rateScale` to 0 when it dies; a stopped transient ends when its particles are gone. At most 256 live per `ParticleEffects` (Mustafar's lightning chain peaks between about 75 and 140); `ParticleEffects.status` counts what was skipped. A light dust storm, the forest's falling leaves and Mustafar's lightning draw nothing except what they carry. `status` asks for the sky again while a sky's effects carry one the pack has no file for.
- **A carried effect named `.swh` is a ribbon, not a particle effect** (`tools/swg/swoosh.mjs`, `src/world/swooshTrail.ts`). An entertainer's ribbon stick is its mesh and one quad written with alpha 0 for its whole life, and that quad exists only to carry the ribbon, so while every carried effect was read as a `.prt` the ribbon failed with "expected FORM PEFT, got FORM SWSH" and the stick was bare. The file is `FORM SWSH > 000N { FORM PTEX, 0000 }`: the particle texture, then RGBA, a width in metres, an effect's name, nine fields nothing reads yet (written into the pack in file order) and, from 0001, a rate, a point count and a subdivision -- all 93 retail files read to the byte (89 before SOE's final names list), and that last reading is ours, made because it gives each effect a sensible length where reading the first unread float as a length would have made the skill-up swirl eighty seconds long. Told apart from a flip-book shader by its extension, never its root tag (an `.sht` can be `FORM SWSH` too). At run time a ribbon is a `SwooshTrail` on a carried handle: its carrier moves it like any carried effect, it keeps a ring of points, cuts its tail exactly at its length, curves each span (Catmull-Rom), and writes a camera-facing strip -- V down its length, U across, which is how the game's gradient textures are laid out -- into the particle batch its texture already has, so it makes no material, program or draw call. Every number of ours is `SWOOSH_TUNE`, live through `__debug.ribbons`; `status` asks for the weapons again while a ribbon its effects carry has no file.
- A flip-book shader is a root `SWTS` form: `NAME` the base shader (sometimes an exporter path like `c:\a_exported\shader\x.sht`, which lives at `shader/x.sht`), a timing form and one `TEXT` per frame (slot tag, texture path) that replaces the base's main texture. The timing forms are the client's switcher classes (their RTTI names are in the executable): `DTST` DeltaTimeSwitcher (count, seconds per frame), `DRTS` DeltaRandomTimeSwitcher (count, min and max, drawn per frame), `DPPT` DeltaPingPongSwitcher (count, min and max; plays forward then back), `DFST`/`DRFS` frame switchers only on samples. `SWSH` is the same with whole shaders as frames; the Theed `SWSH` frames are not in the archives, and the falls the world places are the scrolling `a_splitalpha_scroll` shaders.
- Texture scrolling is `TSNS` (slot tag, then u1, v1, u2, v2 per second) and only matters when the effect's vertex program adds `textureScroll` (`a_scroll.vsh`, `a_scroll_rgb1_a2.vsh`, the water programs): the offset is `frac(rate x seconds)`. Split alpha (`a_splitalpha.psh`) takes colour from set 0 and alpha from set 1 of the same texture; the converter writes an opaque colour image and an alpha-as-grey image (three's `alphaMap` reads green), each on its own glTF sampler so that GLTFLoader, which shares a `Texture` per image and sampler, gives every scrolling material its own offset.
- An effect pass's DATA has a heat byte after dither from version 10 on (and a pixel-shader byte first before version 5): reading blend and test at fixed offsets made every version-10 effect a cut-out. Only the scrolling, split-alpha, flip-book and unlit-alpha-screen shaders among the 2,113 that blend and test with z-write off are converted as translucent, in a new `translucent` field that leaves `alphaMode` alone because the sky's celestial sprites read it; the rest (foliage, hair, fences) stay cut-outs, because three sorts transparency per object and an instanced field would draw far over near. Translucent placed surfaces take `renderOrder` 3, or the terrain water, which follows the player and so always sorts nearer than an instanced mesh's centre, is blended over them. A shader can write its effect inline (`FORM EFCT` inside the shader, the whitewater and a few dozen holograms, particles and UI shaders); its pass is read like a file's.
- In the client a fully masked emissive texel is the texture colour exactly: the `a_emismap` family lerps the lit texture toward the unlit one, and the rest add the mask inside a light that `saturate` caps. three adds `emissiveMap` after lighting, so the converter splits such a texture in linear light into a base colour `rgb x (1 - m)` and an emissive `rgb x m` (image names carry the mask's source); laying `rgb x m` over the full texture made every lit sign twice as bright by day. The mask is read from the pixel program, and two shapes misled: `a_specmap_bump_emismap` declares a local `sample` in two blocks, the diffuse map (its alpha the gloss) and then the normal map, whose alpha is the glow, so a local is resolved from the read nearest before `emisMask`; and the envmask assembly programs `lrp` toward the environment cube (ENVM) by their shine mask with the same instruction the emismap ones use toward the texture, so only an `lrp` toward a sampler on MAIN is a glow. `a_emis_full` and its alpha variants are unlit (`KHR_materials_unlit`, `userData.unlit`); `emisadd` is unlit and additive, with alpha forced opaque only when the source factor is One. The game applies flags that are in a program's key (`alphaTest`, `alphaMap`, additive blending, `fog`) in the GLTF plugin's `afterRoot`, before anything compiles.
- Animated surfaces (`src/world/surfaces.ts`) swap `map`/`emissiveMap` between textures of one kind and move `texture.offset`, neither of which is in three's program key; every GLTFLoader that loads converted static models must be `surfaces.withPlugin(new GLTFLoader())`, and a material's link to its flip-book is the string `userData.swgTrack`, never a texture, because `material.clone()` copies `userData` through JSON. The plugin only registers; a material joins its track in `World.adoptMaterials`, when it is in the scene, and leaves on its `'dispose'`, because `AssetPack` swaps a cell's original material for a clone and never disposes the original. `World.unload` sweeps the records no material joined. The clock runs on `World.simTime`, so `__debug.advance` steps it.
