# Lessons: The network and the server

Peer bodies and hulls, boarding peers, the relay and its words, claims, the world file, groups, shots between players, credits, a peer's saber, another player's vehicle drawn by the motion they send.

Moved here from CLAUDE.md's "Things that took a long time to learn" on 2026-11-19, word for word and in their old order. Add a new lesson about this area here, never to CLAUDE.md. A code comment that cites "CLAUDE.md" or "the lesson in CLAUDE.md" means one of these files: grep `docs/lessons/` for its words.

- Another player's body is a sensor in a collision group of its own that collides with nothing (`Group.peer`, membership the peer bit and a filter of zero): the engine's rule is that a query sees a collider only when the query's filter accepts the collider's membership and the collider's filter accepts the query's, so every query that passes an interaction group misses a peer while a query that passes none is not group-tested at all and finds it — which is exactly the bolts, the blades and the aim rays. A sensor is never in the solver, so a body that teleports between messages can never shove a ship or a crate. Two things measured rather than assumed: rapier's character controller does **not** skip sensors (it stops dead on one the groups let through), so `Physics.isPeer` is a real second line and not decoration; and a capsule of zero radius does not throw but hands back a handle that is not a handle, so the live knob floors both shape numbers. The queries that pass no group at all are, almost all of them, the ones meant to find a body — a blade's sweep, an aim line, a hitscan, a flame cone — with three that are not: `Physics.groundDistance` is the one whose answer is a surface to stand on (it skips peers itself, or a peer standing under a speeder is its road), and the turrets' line of sight and `World.clearGround`'s set-down probe each pass `groups(Group.all, Group.all)` so a player standing in the way neither quiets a turret nor moves where a ship lands. Nothing on this side ever takes health off a peer: a blow goes out through `RemoteBodies.onBlow` and the player who was shot is the only one who subtracts — which is also why a peer joins `World.targets()` only while a blow struck here could reach their browser, since the local wildlife would otherwise chase a picture it can never finish.
- A hull another player flies is made a place to stand in on demand (`src/net/remoteInterior.ts`), never for a ship merely in view. Its rooms hang under the peer's own picture, which is the only thing carrying that hull's pose off the wire, so they ride it with nothing copied and `hullCarrier` answers for whoever is in them by walking parents. The hull itself is a stand-in `Vehicle` that does nothing -- no model, no colliders, held, ghosted, weightless, not in `world.vehicles`, its body written from the picture each frame, its group marked `userData.noBox` so the rooms never grow the box a bolt stops against -- because `WalkableRoom.vehicle` is read as a Vehicle at some thirty places outside the rooms; `InteriorHost` in `interior.ts` is the little the rooms themselves need, and is where that stand-in would be dropped. A hull that is its own portal building has its cells copied out of the picture (`Garage.visualRooms`: geometry and materials shared, every mesh marked `shared`, cell 0 left out since the picture already draws the shell), and the copy is handed back **turned off**, because hanging it, measuring it and preparing it all yield to the frame loop and it is under a picture that is being drawn; a hull with an interior model of its own loads it hidden and prepared. The rooms are stepped by a sweep of the rooms themselves, called once a frame by the game and again by the peers' pass (a second call within `minStep` does nothing), never per peer: a room outlives the player whose hull it was, and a room nothing steps is a floor that has stopped under the person standing on it. A room somebody is standing in is never freed under them: it is cut loose from the picture and left where the hull was, still stepped, until they step out.
- A player standing in a hull somebody else flies sends **which** hull and where they stand in it, never a copy of the hull: a state carries `veh` or `in { ship, p, h }` and `cleanRide` (`server/vehicleWire.mjs`) keeps one of them, taking a browser that sends both at its word about the hull, because the copy is the one thing that must not go out — two crew each sending their own hull had every other browser build two ships in the same place, with two sets of glows and trails and two of everything to prepare and paint. The place is in the hull's own frame, which is what `Player.pos` and `Player.heading` already are aboard, and the figure is carried onto the hull's live pose **after** every clamped picture has been put on what it rides (`placeAboard` beside `placeDocked` in `src/net/remotePlayers.ts`): a world place eased on its own would swim a passenger about the cabin and out through the walls, because the figure and the hull arrive from two players on two clocks. Nothing answers "whose hull is this" in play yet — `hullCarrier` returns 0 for every hull, since a hull somebody else flies is not a place you can stand in — so the branch is reached only by `__aboard({ carrier: <a peer's id> })`; whatever makes a peer's hull boardable must hang its rooms on that peer's own picture, or `hullCarrier` is the one method to change.
- Boarding a hull another player flies goes through a registry, never an import: `setPeerRooms`/`peerRooms` in `docking.ts` is the seam, the wiring in `main.ts` registers an adapter over `World.remoteRooms()`, and nothing in the docking or the menu code knows what builds a room. Which player a room belongs to is a `WeakMap<WalkableRoom, number>` filled as each room is handed over (`idOf`), so the answer is one lookup and an entry dies with the room rather than with a relay number that will be handed out again. Every way out of their rooms says `aboard(id, false)`, which is what clears the builder's `held` and lets the room be freed, and `close(id)` refuses outright for the room the walker is standing in, because freeing a physics world under a body is the one mistake here that is not survivable; `peerHullGone(id)` is the call that must come **before** such a world is freed, and it takes the body out and stands the figure clear. Their ship stays theirs while you are in it: `shipStatus` answers null aboard a peer's room so the ship menu opens nothing there, `handleMount` reads which side the room is before the pilot-spot branch so E can never take their controls, and `eject` refuses, since `eject` runs `travel` and a travel from in there would unload the world around a body that lives in a room's own physics. E at the way in is the way back out. `BOARD_TUNE` (`reach` 8 m, `wait` 20 s) is ours and lives through `__board()`.
- Credits are **a number on a character and not a thing in a backpack** (`server/purse.mjs`, `src/net/purse.ts`), which is the owner's call and is also what the game did: nothing in the archives gives credits an object template. The one rule both halves exist for is that a balance may not be spent twice, so **with a server this side never takes money off**: a spend is asked for, the server's answer is what may be acted on, and the thing that was paid for happens in the callback the answer fires -- a browser that subtracted first and told the server afterwards would be a browser deciding what it can afford. A spend that would go below zero moves **nothing at all** rather than going as far as it can, and a refusal clears what was waiting so a later word cannot set off a journey that was already refused. With no server the number lives on the saved character exactly as its clothes do. The fares are the game's; the starting amount, the cap and the rate are ours, in `PURSE_TUNING`. Nothing in the game earns credits yet, so the starting amount is the whole of anybody's money and is set to cover a good many fares rather than to be a challenge.
- A shot between two players is decided on the shooter's screen and subtracted only on the screen of whoever was hit (`src/net/combatNet.ts`, `server/combatWire.mjs`): everybody is drawn ten times a second and glided in between, so the two screens never agree exactly, someone has to be right, and having the one who was hit take the number off means nobody can ever disagree about how much of anybody is left or that somebody is dead. Every other browser flies its own copy of the bolt `inert` — drawn, heard, whining past the ear, able to be turned away by a blade, and never reaching `damage`, `takeBolt`, `onPlayerHit` or the bolt's own `onHit`, or one trigger pull would be paid for twice — and an inert copy's ray passes over every peer's body as well, since the muzzle it left is inside the shooter's own. Where a bolt stopped is not each browser's to work out: each traces its own streamed world, so the one who fired sends the point and every other cuts its copy short there (`Bolts.cutShort`), which is what puts the mark and the burst in the same place on both screens. A blade turning one of those bolts away is a word and not a second bolt: today's block makes the bolt the local player's own and is local-player only, so on the shooter's screen the blocker is a peer capsule and the deflection would never happen there at all — the blocker's `blocked` cancels that shot on every screen, the shooter's own real bolt included, and the blocker fires its own from the block point, one bolt in and one bolt out. A death is announced by the one who died, with whoever struck inside the last eight seconds, and plays as a clip, never a ragdoll (nothing carries bone motion across, so a ragdoll would end in a different heap on every screen). Health goes over five times a second from a timer and only when it has moved, never from a frame, and is what fills the group roster's bars. Damage between players is the server's own switch (`--friendly-fire`), off unless it is asked for, with the game's own duel inside it (`COMBAT_DUEL` and `COMBAT_PEACE` at the game's own 128 m); the browser refuses to send a blow it knows cannot land and the server drops it too, so a browser on another build cannot hurt anybody in a peaceful world either.
- Another player's lightsaber is a `SaberBlade` like the player's and every fighter's, drawn from the holder already hanging on their hand bone and joining the frame's blade list through `collectBlades`'s `more` list, so with the blade glow pass on it lights the world through that one pass exactly as the player's does. With the pass off it is not the same: the pool is four flash lights, the player's own blade, the fighters' glows and a ship's room lights all ask earlier in the frame and a flash holds its light for its whole life, so a peer's blade (which asks last) takes only one standing dark and often goes unlit. A second `SaberBlade` builds no program: three keys a shader stage on its source text, and the player's own blade groups are in the scene from the start with their ribbons hidden, so `compileAllAsync`'s `traverse` builds that program behind every session's loading screen. Which blades a peer has out is read from what is in their hands and never from a style on the wire, and which hand a holder is on is the hand it names, never its place in the list (the peers skip an empty hand). A peer who leaves hands their renderers on to the next peer rather than disposing them: a blade's materials are in the portal renderer's set and the cascades' map, and people churn all evening.
- **A world key off the wire is kept byte for byte, NUL and all, and a body every browser stands for itself is shared by saying it has been seen.** The server keys a world as its planet, a NUL and its zone (`roomKey` in `server/rooms.mjs`); the browser read a spawn row's world through `readWords`, which strips every control character, so `endor\0` came out `endor` and every record an admin stood was refused on every browser as another world's. A key is only ever compared, never shown or parsed, so `readRow` in `src/net/owned.ts` keeps it as sent. And the world's seeded bodies (a lair's creature `wild:`, a person at a post `stood:`, a nest `camp:`) are named the same in every browser and never sent to anybody: a browser says `seen` when it has stood one and `unseen` when it puts its body down, and the server offers it only to a browser with a body for it (`seers`), however far off, since every browser that has one stood it because it was near. A seen one that died stays dead for its row's own respawn, told with the death as `back` seconds, and is then forgotten so the next browser stands it whole; one nobody keeps or sees for two minutes is forgotten too; a `seen` for one already down is answered to that browser alone with `fresh`, so its copy goes quietly instead of playing a death nobody saw or counting as a kill at its post. A grant handed back must tell the browser to let go (`keep { drop }`), or it goes on keeping what it gave back, alive on its own screen and frozen on every other; an unattackable body (a ticket collector, one of ours) is never shared at all, and a follower leaves sharing with `taken`.
- A group crosses together as a set of separate crossings, never as one: the leader's own `travel` and the jump's countdown both call `TravelTogether.leaving`, which offers the rest of the group the same trip, and whoever says yes makes the crossing their own way (flying in space after a jump they jump too, which is the only crossing that keeps a hull, its rooms and whoever is walking them; anywhere else the ordinary travel with the ship they are flying carried across). Where the leader came out is known only to their own browser -- a `state` goes to the world its player is standing on and no further -- so one word of its own, `cross`, goes to the sender's group and only to the group: `going` as a crossing begins, `here` with the place when it ends. `going` is also what makes the last place stale, so a group returning to a world it has been to before is never sent to the point somebody came out at the time before. The follower waits for that word under its own loading screen, bounded (`waitMs`) and before anybody steps out of a ship, and comes out at that place with a fixed step to one side taken from their own member id, so two ships never arrive inside each other. Whether a trip can be kept at all is asked **before** the yes goes out, because the server counts an acceptance once and for all and one spent on a refusal could never be said again; a member standing in a hull they are not flying is asked to step out first rather than carried, since nothing carries a passenger out of another browser's rooms.
- The relay grew into a small server in place, and every part of it had to be added without breaking a browser built before it. The server sends `hail` the instant a socket opens (the clock, how long a day is, a nonce, whether a join word is wanted, whether damage between players is on); an old browser has no case for it and drops it silently, and `welcome` stays exactly where it always was, sent once in answer to the first hello — a browser that ran `case 'welcome'` twice would re-fire `onJoin` for every peer and re-fetch a rig, a look and a `prepare` for each of them mid-play. A browser that hears no hail within a second knows it is talking to the relay that came before and turns everything new off. Adding fields to `welcome` is safe; sending it twice is not. The version a hello says is the other half of the same promise, and it bit when the wire went from three to four: the third wire's relay records a browser's version only when it is exactly its own and passes a creature's bite on only to a browser it recorded, so a newer browser that said 4 in a hello sent before the hail was recorded as nothing and lost every bite on itself while `speaks(3)` still said yes. A hello now says the lesser of the browser's version and the server's once the server has hailed (`Session.helloVersion`), a hello that went first is said again in the server's version when that is the third wire or later, and every relay since the fourth takes the lesser of the two itself (`Math.min` where it records `c.v`), so a browser and a relay a wire apart keep everything the older of the two speaks and lose only what is new.
- A claim can fail in two quite different ways and they must not be answered alike. A proof that does not match and a wrong join word are the end of the line: that browser is not who it says it is, asking again cannot change that, and it is sent `denied` and closed. A character that belongs to another player is the end of that **character** only: the browser is sent `refused`, keeps its line and can offer a different one. A character id can genuinely collide, because the maker falls back to a time plus six random characters on a page with no `crypto.randomUUID`, and closing the line over it would lock that player out of the server for good, on every reconnect, with nothing telling them why.
- The server's news goes only to the world a player is standing on, but a peer is taken apart only when their line actually closes. `join` is the expensive word — `RemotePlayers.remove` then `add` → `dress`, a rig fetch, the wearables and `World.prepareActor`, which is a shader compile on a live frame — and `hello` is the cheap one, which the browser already handles correctly when it says another planet: keep the peer, stop drawing them. So the server keeps a set per connection of the peers that browser holds, sends `hello` for anyone it holds and `join` only for a first meeting, never sends `leave` merely because someone travelled, and sends `leave` on a closing line to everyone who holds that peer wherever they are now standing. What is saved is the stranger on another planet, who is never mentioned at all; a friend hopping to another planet and straight back costs nothing, which is what the first cut of the routing got wrong.
- The world the server keeps is `world.json` plus `world.log` (a line of JSON per change with a sequence number, appended and `fsync`ed as it happens; the snapshot is written to a temp file, flushed, renamed over the real one, and only then is the log emptied). Three Windows facts decide its shape: `ftruncateSync` on a handle opened for appending throws `EPERM`, so the log is emptied by closing the handle, writing the file empty and opening it again; a rename over a file another program holds open throws `EPERM`/`EBUSY`, so it is tried a few times and then left to the next ordinary write with the log keeping everything; and a failed rename must schedule nothing of its own, or the server writes and fsyncs the whole world about fifteen times a second for as long as somebody has the file open in an editor. Every table keyed by something a browser chose is `Object.create(null)`, and is rebuilt that way when a file is read back, so `__proto__` is a key and not a way into the language's own objects; the wire refuses `__proto__`, `constructor` and `prototype` outright as the near half of the same belt.
- A server started with a join word listens to nothing but a claim until the browser has said who it is (`relay.mjs`), so the hello cannot go first: with a word set the browser waits for the hail, claims, and then says hello, and with no word it says hello at once exactly as it always did. A browser told its character was opened somewhere else must clear `wanted` as well as closing the socket, or the reconnect takes the character back and the two browsers kick each other for ever. The player's key is never sent: what a server keeps is a second hash of it (`server/identity.mjs`), and what answers the server's challenge is an HMAC of the nonce under that.
- **`receive()` in `src/net/net.ts` is a switch with no default: a server word with no `case` there is dropped in silence.** The server's `purse` answer had none for as long as the purse existed, so with a server every fare waited for ever and the balance read nought while every purse test passed (they handed the purse its words by hand). `tools/swg/tests/netRoutes.test.ts` now reads every server module for the words it sends (anything spelled `t: '<word>'` that is not a `write(`/`.change(` record) and fails for one with no case, and the relay's header must list it too. A test of anything the server says goes through the browser's own `Net` against a real relay (`purse.test.ts`, `storyRelay.test.ts`), never only through the module's own `word()`. And a tie between two copies of a character that is answered from memory is answered *before* the `claimed` word arrives (the server sends `settle` first), so whatever waits on the tie must ask `session.ask` (is a question still standing?) and never `keep === 'ask'`, which the claim reports even when the answer has already gone; the relay keeps the verdict on the line (`c.keep`, rewritten by `onSettle` and by the wait running out) for whatever settles after the character. Two more traps of the same settling: a part of the character that `characterMark` reads and that a settle moves (the story book's `local`, which a settle takes back to nought) must adopt its new mark through `Session.noteSettled`, which moves no counter, since a settle counted as a change makes this browser's copy the newer one at the next claim, written over a server's copy the player had just chosen; and nothing that settles may speak before the line has the claim (`authority === 'server'`), or a word left over from the last line reaches the server after the claim and is answered for a tie the player has not yet been shown (the server refuses a `sync` while `c.asking` stands, as the near half of the same belt).
- A group is held by the server, and a member of it is a **character, not a connection**: the key is `c:<character id>` once a browser has claimed one and `s:<connection>` otherwise, which is what lets a reload keep its place. A line closing holds that place for ninety seconds — the group is told the member stepped out, the roster reads them away, the place still counts against the eight so a reload cannot be locked out of its own group, and the leader changes hands only when the hold runs out, to whoever has been in the group longest. The one closing that must not look like stepping out is a takeover: the same character opened in a second browser closes the older line, so `Groups.absent` ignores a line that is no longer the one holding the member and `present` takes the older line out the moment the newer one claims, or the whole group is told somebody went away who is standing right there. Turning an invitation down takes back that one invitation and no other — only somebody actually going takes the ones they sent with them, and whoever sent one is told, rather than being left reading that it is still out while the person they asked has already been dropped.
- The group's words and its distances are the game's, read out of `datatables/player/radial_menu.iff`: an invitation reaches 90 m (`GROUP_INVITE`), a trade 8 m, a duel 128 m and a death blow 10 m, and 16384 m is that table's mark for no limit rather than a distance, so an invitation to somebody on another world is refused for being on another world and never for being 16,384 metres away. `GROUP_RANGES` in `server/groups.mjs` and `GROUP_RANGE` in `src/net/groups.ts` each hold the rows and neither is tunable. Everything beside them is ours and is one object apiece — how many fit in a group, how long an invitation stands, how many one browser may have out, how long a place is held, how often it may ask its group for anything, how many lines of chat a second — printed by the status page and moved for a run with `--set group.<name>=<number>`. A chat line is sent back to the speaker by the server rather than shown where it was typed, so everyone reads the same words in the same order and what is shown is what the server let through; a distance is measured in the relay, which is the only part that knows where two players are standing, and the state a browser sent from a world it has since left is thrown away rather than measured against.
- A label over another player's head hangs on where their figure is being **drawn** this frame (`RemotePlayers.peerAnchor`), never on where their last message put them: the figure glides toward that point over the tenth of a second between messages, and one riding in somebody else's hull is placed from the carrier's own pose, so a label hung on the message's place swims off the head and snaps back ten times a second. The roster's distances and the invitation's 90 m read the message's own place, where that lag does not matter.
- **Another player's vehicle is drawn by the motion their own game sends, and whatever rides that picture takes the very same step, from the very same record, at the very same moment** (Racer wave 4a: `src/net/peerReckon.ts`, `cleanMotion` in `server/vehicleWire.mjs`, `RemotePlayers.update`, the reckoned branch of `HullFrames.stepPeer`). A `veh` carries `vl` (the sender's velocity), `yr` (its turn about straight up) and `at` (when its place was true on the shared clock, `placeMoment`), all three or none, and the hail says `veh: 2`; glided as before, a full pod was drawn 22 to 40 m behind itself. **A state goes out before its frame's own physics step** (`stepNet`, then `Physics.step`), so the place it carries is the one the frame before's step left: its moment is the frame's start on the shared clock less the frame's own `dt`, which the bodies have not taken yet, and less `Physics.behind`. The first cut took off only the remainder, and every picture stood the sender's speed over its frame rate behind (2.8 m for a full pod at 60 fps, 5.6 at 30), which no test could see because both modelled the sender the other way round (step, then send) and the smoke measured against the sender's own log: a stand-in of a sender sends before it steps, as `App` does (peerReckon's `simulate`, grappleVehicles' `PeerStandIn`, each with a `late` stamp the tests show failing). A turn the race drive or the flight writes this frame is a frame ahead of that place too, and is carried back over the frame's step (`turnBack`) to the turn the body stood at beside it; their velocity is not, being the one the coming step moves the body by. A race drive paused while play stands still sends no turn rate, or every other screen drew it turned up to fourteen degrees off its still hull. The picture moves along the arc the sent velocity and turn carry it (carried straight it stood 3.8 m outside a pod's hardest turn) and eases (`RECKON_TUNE.ease`) toward the state's place carried by the state's age, which is read on the shared clock and never from when the state was heard, so the wire's jitter and a busy frame move nothing; no motion, no clock shared, or an age when heard past `early`/`trust` glides as before, and a state is carried no further than `most`, so a peer gone quiet stands rather than sliding back. **The moment is read once, at the frame's top** (`reckonAt`, before the player's update), and handed to the rope (`RemoteBodies.stampReckon`) and to the pictures (`RemotePlayers.reckonAt`) alike, and **the record the rope reads is the ride's own, by reference** (`PeerView.shipReck`, `PeerHullBox.reck`): a state is heard between frames and the picture is reckoned from it in the next, so a copy taken as the list was last refilled handed the rope the state before on every frame one came. grappleVehicles' test 13 holds the pose the rope ends a frame on to the picture reckoned after it to the bit, under a vehicle whose speed surges so two states are two predictions, and parts them by 0.01 to 0.5 m with either fault (the frame before's moment, the copy); every tow on the smooth pose there is held to the trace the code at the wave's start made of it (`PRE_WAVE_TOWS`, c862036), since its check that the smooth path had not moved compared two runs of the new code; and the game's own hand-overs (the moment, the record, the stamp, the turn carried back, the nudge's list, a group run's shared start and its words to the group) are pinned as text in grappleWiring, raceTrack and raceDrive's section 25, because every behaviour test makes them for itself. The figure rides the reckoned picture in the ride's own frame (`rideOn`) instead of gliding after it, and the motion blur, a pod's gait and `vehiclePose` read the velocity sent while a picture is reckoned. Numbers go to the reckoning in kept records (`ARC_IN`, `TURN_B`, the rope's `rn`): handed `carriedIn` and `slerpInto` as arguments they made 2.6 B a step. Measuring the rope on a reckoned pose found an old hole of the rope's own: hung at a peer's rear face the fan met the box on every frame, and the search after it handed `fanOf`, `clearAt` and `cast` its numbers as arguments, about a kilobyte a frame in two processes of three whenever the compiler left them out of `sweep` (the young generation's count read 13 B a frame only because 28 scavenges had emptied it, which is why a window with any collection measures nothing); they go in `fq` and `cs` now. **A group race is words and every browser's own run** (`src/net/raceNet.ts`, `src/race/raceGroup.ts`, `server/raceWire.mjs`, hail `race: 1`): the leader's `call` lists the members by relay id in grid order and the start on the shared clock, the relay carries `race` to the sender's group alone as it carries `cross`, a member's slot is its place in that list behind the grid, and its countdown holds to the shared start until go (`RaceRun.sharedStart`, the remaining time rounded up to whole physics steps) and runs on its own steps from there, so a group's go is one instant and each member is timed as a run alone is; laps and finishes go out as words and every browser works the same standings out of them. The nudge between pods (`RaceDrive.near`, filled from `RemotePlayers.nearPictures` before the hulls step, `nudgeReach`, `nudgeStick`) costs nothing with nobody near and allocates nothing with somebody near (`raceAllocOwn.ts` runs a mirrored pair in and out of reach so the weave still crosses its gates).
