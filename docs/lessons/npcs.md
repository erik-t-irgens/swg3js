# Lessons: NPCs and creatures

Mobiles, fighters, the AI fixes, indoor pathing, ragdolls, the living, perched people, the emulator's rows and screenplays, camps, the NGE overlay.

Moved here from CLAUDE.md's "Things that took a long time to learn" on 2026-11-19, word for word and in their old order. Add a new lesson about this area here, never to CLAUDE.md. A code comment that cites "CLAUDE.md" or "the lesson in CLAUDE.md" means one of these files: grep `docs/lessons/` for its words.

- A mobile's animations are shared: every appearance with the same skeletons and animation table plays one pack (`mobiles/anims/<pack>.glb`), so a clip is baked once for the hundreds of templates that use it, and the pack's JSON names the clip for each role rather than the game guessing by name. The tables nest their selectors (a mount's branch outside a mood's), and the shipped flattener loses the inner one, so `mobiles` has its own path-keeping flattener and picks the wild, calm, unridden branch: a bantha walks at 0.86 and runs at 3.47 m/s, not the ridden 6.93. Packs are compacted on the way out (no track for a joint that never leaves its bind pose, two keys for one that holds still), which is about half the bytes, and a one-frame pose is written twice a frame apart because three finishes a zero-length clip on its first update and poses a repeating one at NaN.
- **SOE's own people for Kashyyyk and Mustafar are an overlay laid over the world's wildlife pack after everything else, stood from a committed reference of their rows by every converter** (`tools/swg/nge.mjs` reads a copy, `tools/swg/ngeref.mjs` writes and reads the reference `tools/swg/ngeref/`, `tools/swg/ngepack.mjs` rules, `src/world/ngeOverlay.ts` fetches and merges; SOE complete, waves 3 and 6). `spawns` writes `assets-private/nge/` from the reference with no flag (the owner's decision of 2026-10-19 that these rows ship for every client: the hard rules' second exception); `--nge=<folder>` reads a live copy, its target refused where git would take it, a copy inside the repository refused, an `@` value refused (the cli expands `@NAME` from `.env` before it parses, so the word is read off the raw command line first, `ngeArg`); and `--nge=none` leaves them out, an index saying so in their place. Where an overlay from a copy may be written is asked of the repository that really holds the folder, from where it really is (`gitPlace`: the part that exists carried through any junction, and git asked from there), because git asked from this worktree about a path in a sibling checkout answers only "outside this repository", which read as "no repository" let a worktree write into a sibling checkout's tracked `src`, and a worktree's `assets-private` is a junction into another checkout that is that checkout's to ignore. The code names the copy's table paths, its column and objvar names and the few generic spawner scripts and templates a row's kind is told by (format knowledge, as `core3.mjs` names Core3's fields): which spawners a script stands rather than the data (`gated`) is told by shape -- shaped as a spawner, not a building, and naming nobody in `strSpawns` -- never by a list of the copy's own template names. Its rule tests still write a tree of `test_*` names into the temporary folder at run time and read every file back for a stray name; the reference's own tests read the committed reference and print counts. Every name the overlay writes is `nge:`-prefixed and the merge refuses an overlay that names anything else (`strayName`), and its people go **after** Core3's rows and the heralds (`standingList`), because `stood:<world>:<n>` is a place in that list and every other browser must keep naming everybody else the same; a world Core3 holds nothing of starts from an empty pack named for the overlay's world, or every row is `stood:here:`. Its creature numbers go through `gameOf` by way of `ngeMob`, so a level means one thing whichever source stood it, and a spawner more than `NGE_RULES.lift` over the pack's ground (a canopy walkway) stands its people in a still knot on itself, since a body drawn across its disc perches on nothing and nothing of ours walks one along a walkway. **A row is named by its `objid` only where its table writes one**: some areas' tables have no such column and every row reads nought there, and keyed by that nought every spawner of the area drew the same creature at the same offset, respawn and heading and stood under one shared name, so such a row is named by a hash of what it says with its twins counted (`rowIds`, as the crews' rows are; the text itself carries the row's scripts and stays out of the reference). Its patrollers walk their rounds whatever they may be fought (`walks`, `setGoing` beside `walksRound`), since the server's own patrol spawner walked its fighters, where Core3's towns left their combat walkers at their posts. Five traps. `WildLife.load` parses Core3's JSON only where it will use it (a world the index does not list reads exactly what it read before, parse and all). `status` asks for `spawns` on the to-do list a launcher runs (which names no `--nge`) wherever a zone of theirs is converted without the reference's rows, told by the index, its reference's hash, its catalogue and its layouts, and asks for an overlay read from a live copy only by hand. The overlay stands everywhere by default, a server's world included, as the world's seeded bodies, each under its own key (`stood:<world>:<key>`, `stoodName` in `standingPeople.ts`) and never its place in the list: the converter drops a row whose creature joins no body of that machine's own catalogue, and by place every row after it would name another body in a browser whose catalogue joins it (Core3's rows keep their places, which every build since they were shared names them by); `where` set to `alone` (a comparison) leaves it out where a server others share holds the world (`serverHoldsFor`: the props keeper's rule from an arrival, the relay that came before counted as shared, and a session's first world with a server saved counted as held, because its connect comes only after that loading screen and nothing can answer before it lifts -- waiting on it would hold the screen for good). `WildLife.retune` lays the world out again from the merged pack it holds and must carry the overlay's people across, which `adopt` empties. And **a world's arrival point is ours and can land in SOE's own hostile camp**: the flattest spot near a layout centre stood, on Mustafar, among aggressive people the data placed there, and with no cloning facility on that world the respawn came round at the same spot and died again. So the overlay's people and herds who pick a fight on sight are kept `NGE_OVERLAY_TUNE.arrivalClear` (64 m, ours, live) clear of where the game brings a player in and back (`World.arrivalPoint`, the App's own `spawn`, which a teleport moves too), by their spot, their roam, every point of their round and a herd's spread, and one already standing there when that place moves onto it is put down; nobody else's row is touched. Mustafar itself now arrives on the Mensix Mining Facility's landing deck (wave 6, the lesson below), and the clear follows its spawn there.
- Indoor pathing (`src/world/nav/`) is ours, no library, and it degrades in three steps rather than two: null outdoors and for anything flying; the doorway polygons alone, which are a room-to-room graph the pack has always carried and which needs no reconversion; and the real corners once a cell has a floor. A body walks a path by what it **faces** and never by its goal point, because `moveTo` is also the arrival test and a body given each corner as its goal stops a stride short of every one and never reaches the one after. Three things are not obvious. The converter's adjacency is checked and not believed (taken only when it is exactly three per triangle and every link points back across the same two corners, else rebuilt from the shared edges), since a floor read wrongly is a body walking through a wall with nothing to say so. A path is keyed on the loaded model **and** the placed building, or two copies of one building down the street from each other share corners and a body walks at the other one's. And a lift shaft is named by `lifts.ts`'s own `LIFT_CELL` and no route ever goes through one: a shaft's stops are doorways with nothing between them, so a route through it walks a body into the air, and now that a fighter falls rather than being teleported that is a visible fall. Every invented number is `NAV_TUNE` in `navMesh.ts`, live through `__debug.nav`; the agent radius is not ours, it is the player's own 0.35 and the node test fails if `player.ts` and the table disagree. A body outside a building finds its way in through `src/world/nav/doorway.ts`: each doorway to cell 0 is turned once into two points in the world, a step outside its plane and a step inside it; the door taken is the shortest walk inside (over the room graph, `doorCosts`) plus outside; the body is walked to the outside point on the outdoor grid, through to the inside one, and the building's floors take it from the moment its followed room flips; and a doorway whose sill stands well over or under the ground outside it (a station's door in the air, a dungeon's opening under the sand) is not a way in on foot. Where the goal is, is the caller's to say: a goal nobody can place steers by the old rules exactly.
- A fighter has the player's own character controller and the player's own numbers, so a wall, a step and a slope stop it, which is what finally makes its stuck check fire. Two things had to be decided rather than copied. Outdoors the planet's own height stays a floor it may never go below -- only a floor, so a ramp still lifts it -- because the heightfield colliders exist only within three chunks of the player and a controller alone would drop a wandering fighter out of the world; indoors there is no such floor on purpose, since dungeon rooms go far below the terrain, and a room whose collision the streamer has not built holds the body at the height it had (`LayoutStreamer.cellsSolid`, the `NpcDeps.cellSolid` hook) rather than letting it fall through. The collider is the player's 1.6 m capsule while `FIGHTER_BODY.halfHeight` stays 0.9, because dozens of places read that number to aim at a fighter's middle and the collider and the aim point need not be one number. What stops a fighter is fixed or bodiless colliders only: another fighter, a creature, a mobile, a vehicle, the player, a peer and a corpse all pass through, or a crowd jamming on one another would each report itself stuck and give the fight up. Its stance is the player's three-way carry chosen from what it has to fight rather than from a mouse button, and the barrel is measured against the point the next bolt is going to and folded into the spine by `Player.correctAim`'s own arithmetic; what the spine cannot take goes on the drawn body and never on the heading, which is the brain's. The aim is **held** through the recoil window and a stagger, not eased to nothing, which is the player's own early return (`aimMode` in `fighterStance.ts`): eased, the barrel never settles on anything.
- **The AI handoff's first three steps were faults and not skill, so they are mended at every tier, tier 0 included, and each is a switch back to the code it replaced** (`src/world/aiLedger.ts`: `AI_FIX.walls`, `posture`, `route`, and the ledger that measures them, `__debug.aiLedger`). **A wall test compares rooms and always asks the physics** (`wallBetween`): it compared buildings, so two bodies in two rooms of one building were never apart and swung at each other through the wall between them (in a starport, 34 to 79% of a fight), and two outside were never asked; with no physics handed over it now answers "apart exactly when the rooms differ". **A line from inside a building to somebody on the other side of its shell tests the shell** (`segmentBlocked`'s `full`, which `wallBetween`, both `lineTo`s and the ledger's `seesLine` pass when `acrossShell` says the two ends are in different buildings or in and out): the indoor filter leaves the shells out, which is right for a line that stays inside and let a body just inside a door strike through the jamb at one outside. The shells are trimeshes made with no orientation and so have no inside -- a ray that starts within a wall's thickness meets its far face where it crosses it, measured, never at nought -- so nothing is split at a doorway's plane; a solid shell would need that split. The doorway test of who can reach whom still never sees a shut door, and the line of fire still does (`stillOrWall`). **The player is aimed at the middle of the capsule the player wears, read live off the player and never copied** (`PlayerTarget.capsule`, the player itself handed over once, its `colliderOffsetY`: 0.8 m standing and 0.5 m crouched, kneeling and prone, all three wearing the low capsule), where everything aimed at 0.9 m: a gunner whose target crouched behind a 0.85 m crate saw over it and fired 40 shots of 40 at a body it could not see. Every reader of `halfHeight` as an aim point takes it -- the gunners' lines, aim and shots, the brains' level test, the nameplate, the powers, a hit's middle -- **and the one reader that meant the shooter does not**: the cover search's threat point is where a shot comes *from*, so it reads `shotHeightOf` (`Living.shotHeight`, `PLAYER_SHOT_FROM` on the player and on a peer, the 0.9 it always read), or a crouched player's hip read as the muzzle and a crate the player shoots over read as a hole. A peer is low while the state they send says so (`peerLow`, off the rig state the wire already carried, so the wire is unchanged) and wears the low capsule then, set on its feet (`setHalfHeight`). A table hides nobody lying behind it: the line under its top is open, and a bolt flies under it as the line does. **Every move is routed in every state, and a slide's lean is laid on its goal before the route, never on the corner after it** (`routed` in `npcs.ts`, `routedNow` in `mobile.ts`): a fighter's attack state skipped the route, and the lean of up to 60 degrees rewritten onto the route's corner walked a gunner sliding round a building into its wall (95% of a fight through it, 54 of 56 hits with no line). **A slide whose corner the route chose keeps its gun on what it fights up to the legs' own ceiling (`legMax`), never its tier's lean** (`legsFor`, `src/world/groundMove.ts`, the fighters' and the people's one rule): a room's path always ends at its goal, so indoors every move is "routed" (`via !== moveTo`), and the bearing to the target drifts a few degrees past the lean while the body walks at a corner held between two plans -- held to the lean, the gun swung onto the feet and back at every plan, at tiers 3 to 5. The standoff's try order is one class both use too (`StandoffTries`), because a fighter's copy of it is code node cannot load and no test ran. **A tactical goal is checked before it is chosen** (`walkGoalFits`, `src/world/nav/goalFits.ts`: indoors the room's own floor, `fitsAt`; outdoors the grid's `lineThick` from where the body stands; a standoff point refused tries the slide the other way, then straight in, and is counted once a refusal, `refused`), and a cover spot only where a body can stand on the grid (`standable`, never `lineThick`, which refuses nearly every real cover spot: the route walks round the blocker). Nothing writes a corner into `moveTo`. **The ledger is a ruler no switch moves**: its rays (eight flat rays a thought for brushing, the line to the capsule the player really wears for a hidden shot) read the same before and after, which is the only way one scene measures a step; a person moves only on whole physics steps, so its pushing is judged over `pushWindow`, never a frame (`stepPush`, run at 60 and 144 frames a second in `aiSteps.test.ts`). Two traps in testing the checks: `legInset` subtracts the bake's own side margin (0.35 m under `rules` 2, a whole cell under the old rule), so a person's width never decides a leg and a test that "the body's width counts" needs a body wider than the margin on a `rules` 2 grid; and `standable`'s neighbour rule is exercised only by a blocked cell beside open ground, which a grid with no margin round its solids has only at the solid's edge. In-engine scenes in a town are noisy: the town's own people draw a fighter off, and a body that loses its room leaves by the street and sticks on a join, which is the handoff's later steps' business and not a fault of these.
- A ragdoll (`ragdoll.ts`) is built from any skeleton without a parts table: a capsule per bone long enough, ball joints at the bone origins, and the bones posed back from the bodies each frame (parents first, each local transform read under the parent's fresh world matrix). Its colliders carry `ActiveHooks.FILTER_CONTACT_PAIRS` and `Physics.hooks` keeps only their contacts with fixed or bodiless colliders, so corpses never trip the living; the character controller skips them by predicate. A corpse's own pieces meet each other only while `RAGDOLL.selfCollide` is on, and then never two a joint holds together and never two that already lay inside each other in the pose it died in (both would be thrown apart on the first step), and never the pieces of two different corpses. Rapier's JS `World.step(eventQueue, hooks)` runs the hooks only when an event queue is passed as well: without one they are silently skipped, which had every ragdoll piece colliding with every other and shivering for ever, whatever the springs were set to (`__debug.ragdoll().hooks` counts the calls), so every deliberate step outside the loop goes through `Physics.stepOnce` and never the raw `world.step()`. Nothing inside a contact hook may call into the engine: the world is mid-step, so `world.getRigidBody(handle)` from inside `filterContactPair` throws "recursive use of an object detected", which unwinds out of the filter's own callback rather than anywhere a frame can catch it — the pair is then refused by the accident of the throw and nothing the filter counts is counted. Whether the other body moves on its own is therefore worked out before the step (`Physics.movers`, refreshed only while there is a corpse in the world) and the filter touches nothing but its own two maps. The engine's per-axis joint motors and limits on a ball joint were tried and dropped: the springs are torques applied in `Ragdoll.spring` each frame, fading out after the fall, and a body near still for a second (or slow for a few) is stopped and put to sleep; every number is live through `__debug.ragdoll({...})`. A piece carries its capsule's own angular inertia, three times harder to swing across than to spin about itself (a thin one, twenty), and a wanted angular acceleration is turned into a torque through that piece's own tensor (`jointTorque`) and never through one scalar: through the mean of the three, `RAGDOLL.maxAccel` was 2.4 times itself about an ordinary limb's long axis and the damping was over-applied by the same factor, which on a thin-boned skeleton put the explicit damping term past the step size at which it grows instead of settling. `RAGDOLL.inertiaBlend` at 1 puts back the single ball every piece used to turn as. Aboard, the ragdoll lives in the room's physics with the hull's matrix as its frame. The terrain's `floor` reset (fallen out of the world) must not fire inside a building: dungeon rooms go far below it.
- Everything alive is one kind with one key: the player, the creatures and the fighters all implement `Living` (`src/combat/kit.ts`), the world hands out one list of them (`World.targets()`), and `damage(amount, from, push, source)` carries who struck so that what is hurt can turn on them. Keys start at 2 (`nextLivingKey`) and the player's is a named 1 (`PLAYER_KEY`), so no key is ever 0 and "nobody" can be stored as a falsy number without the player being the one thing that vanishes. Anything with a timer runs on `World.simTime`, which only `stepLiving` advances, so `__debug.advance` exercises it.
- A fighter's body is kinematic and its update writes its height from the terrain every frame, so a Force power that only set a position was undone before it was seen: holding and throwing one takes a branch in that update (held, falling, or on the ground) and a knock hard enough to lift it, not just a `holdAt` method.
- A mobile pack clip may have no track for a joint that never leaves its bind pose: three blends such a property toward the value it had when the binding was made. So nothing poses a mobile's bones before its mixer binds them, and nothing stands a body up again in place: one killed is taken away, and whatever comes back at its post is a new body, cloned, hung and bound fresh, so a ragdoll's pose can never be left on the joints no clip keys (the rest pose a respawn once put back is no longer kept at all).
- The mobile catalogue is fetched once at boot and is usually still in flight when the first planet loads, so everything reads it through a getter (`MobileCatalogue.loaded`, the manager's `catalogue()` dependency) and treats "not yet" as absent. A manager that captured null at load would be dead for the life of that planet.
- A mobile ignores a blow from its own side from one that would never pick a fight with it (`Mobile.damage`): a pack spitting at the player through its own ring otherwise turned on itself and feuded for the rest of the fight. A creature's spit falls, so it is aimed over the target by the drop over its flight.
- A mobile's speed is two numbers, the acceleration's ramp and what the chosen clip's feet show (`stepGait` in `mobiles/gait.ts`): the gait is chosen from the ramp, and the body moves at what the gait shows. Kept as one, the ramp was pulled back every frame to the walk clip's cap (1.8 times its speed) and a two-gait pack whose cap sits under the walk-run midpoint never broke into its run: a bantha chased at 1.5 m/s against its 5.2.
- A mobile's building room is followed through the portals as the player's is (`LayoutStreamer.trackCell`, from the manager four times a second and every 2 m), never read from the rooms' bounds: rooms overhang their hull, and a body on the street beside one was taken to be inside and lost the terrain from its colliders. The terrain-floor reset skips a mobile inside, as it does the player. Mobiles are in `World.targets()` once their model is up (`m.ready`), so the powers, the Bounty Hunter's weapons, the fighters and each other find them.
- The dressed NPCs are mobiles, not fighters: a fighter is stood by the spawner and leashed to where it was stood, and a cantina NPC must follow the player out of the door and back in. A person is a "look" (`mobiles/look.ts`): a parts character (a species body, or the NPC's own `mobiles/models/<app>/parts.json`) built once per entry with `Character.load(..., { dir, skipRig: true })`, dressed with `wearItemFrom(<wardrobe folder>, item, part)`, coloured (the template's `custom` set as shared, which reaches each private copy; each piece's values as `<part>|<name>`), and cloned per spawn after `customizer.settled()`. No rig is fetched: the pack's clips bind to the clone by joint name.
- **A person the data stands on something raised keeps the row's height, and is held there until what they stand on is solid** (`src/world/mobiles/perch.ts`). For two passes an outdoor row was handed over with no height and `World.groundAt` outdoors is the terrain alone, so every Ewok on Endor's tree villages, a sniper on a Corellian bridge and Veers and Thrawn on their Naboo balcony stood on the ground under where they belonged (184 of Endor's 411 outdoor rows are more than 0.3 m over the terrain, 531 over the ground worlds); the pass 6 note that the manager "finds a platform" was never true, and lairs stand on the terrain only because their spot is moved clear of the camp's pieces. Now the height goes to the manager through `perchSpawn`, never below the terrain, and a body more than `lift` over the ground is perched -- held like a body in a room with no floor -- until one probe that ignores the terrain finds its floor (`landTop`; a hit far under the terrain is something buried and counts as nothing). Whether that floor can still come is asked of the objects whose own turned footprint covers the point, never of whole regions (`LayoutStreamer.builtAt` over the pure `floorAt` in `placedTiers.ts`): `built` lets the body down at once, `coming` waits with a safety clock that never counts behind a loading screen, and `far` holds it with no clock while the player is too far for the streamer to build it. A driven body is never perched and is decided again when handed back, and a body whose floor loses its collision while the world runs is perched again (`onFloorGone` to `perchOver`). Every number is `PERCH_TUNE`; `__debug.people({ perch: true })` counts held, far, landed, fell, timed out and perched again. A body knocked off a platform in a fight is still not held.
- Whatever is stood before the pack's terrain arrives -- the people the warm-up stands behind the loading screen (`World.warmUp`), a lair stood on the first passes -- stands on the stand-in heights; when the real ground comes in it can be metres above them. Mobiles are lifted back onto the terrain from the manager's 4 Hz pass (`Mobile.liftToGround`), and a frozen-tier body only sleeps when grounded: one put to sleep in mid-fall hung 200 m under the ground for good.
- **The emulator's rows are in the snapshot's frame, and every reader has to carry them into the world's.** Every placed object goes through `LayoutStreamer`'s mirror -- `gx = -(x - centre.x)`, `gz = z - centre.z` -- and a yaw goes to its negative with it. The lairs always did that (`intoWorld` in `wildLife.ts`); the standing people never did, so for two passes all 4,619 of them stood on the wrong side of their planet: the Tuskens of Fort Tusken on empty sand kilometres off, and nobody indoors at all, since a cantina's floor is not where its mirror image is (measured over five worlds, 541 of Tatooine's 622 rows stood within 30 m of a placed object as stored and none as the runtime read them). It escaped every spot check because `__debug.people({ go })` walked the player to the same wrong place and always found somebody there. `StandingPeople` now keeps the pack's rows raw and carries them once, route points and heading with them, on the first pass that knows the layout's centre (`frame`); a building's own children take `childInWorld` instead. Anything new that reads a converted row with a place in it must say which frame the row is in before it stands anything, and its console helper's `go` must use the same carry as the thing it is checking, or it checks nothing.
- **What a town's screenplay says about the one standing there is the server's, and most of it is not hostile.** Every city row is stood `AI_STATIC` (its brain off: it never wanders and walks home after a fight, `post: 'still'`), a stationary commoner gets `pvp 0` and no AI at all (part of the furniture: `peaceful`, `essential`), a stationary row names nobody and draws four in five from the town's `stationaryCommoners` and one in five from its `stationaryNpcs` at each spawn, a guard's row carries both sides and a world nobody holds stands the Imperial one (`city.lua`'s `spawnGcwMobiles`), and a patroller starts 40 to 60 s after it is stood and lingers 30 to 60 s at a point the route flags, 5 s at any other (`setupMobilePatrol`, `mobileDestinationReached`). Those are the server's; every other number of the standing people -- how near they stand, the two caps of forty, how far a post's person steps about -- is ours, in `PEOPLE_TUNE`.
- **A catalogue body is one creature's numbers out of every creature drawn as it, so a row's numbers come from the row.** The mobiles catalogue takes an entry's stats from the first Core3 mobile that wears its look (`mobiles.mjs`), and looks are shared: 3,445 Core3 creatures are drawn on 1,496 bodies, 1,474 of them differ from their body's level by five or more and 833 disagree with it on temper, and all 35 kinds of Tusken, levels 8 to 263, stood as the level-8 child, 192 health, defensive and unarmed. So the spawns pack (format 2) carries each creature's own `game` block -- level, health, damage, temper, ranged attack, whether it may be struck -- computed with the very `core3StatsFor` the catalogue uses, and its weapon groups, and a standing person, a lair's creature and a camp's body each wear their own creature's numbers over their body's (`overridesOf`, `weaponsOf`, `SpawnOpts.overrides`); the entry answers only for a row whose creature says nothing. Nothing that stands a body for a row may read its level, its temper, whether it may be struck or what it holds from the catalogue entry.
- **A camp is what its point of interest's client data hangs on it, not its template's appearance.** 263 of the 270 theater lairs drew `nests/rock_pebbleclump_a3.glb`, because a camp template's own appearance is a pebble: its tents, cots, stools, terminals, banners and fires are `CHLD`/`CHL2` children of its `clientDataFile` (`clientdata/poi/*.cdf`), the very form `clientfx.mjs` reads for a brazier's fire. All 107 camp templates the lairs name have one, 1,156 children over them, 1,145 of them models, median five pieces and at most thirty, none missing from the retail archives; the spawns command converts them into `spawns/nests/` and writes them as `camps[template]`, and each piece stands on its own ground with a collider of its own, since the template's `terrainModificationFileName` (`poi_small.lay`) flattened ground that cannot be flattened now (the houses lesson) -- which is also why a site too far out of level is refused, as the emulator refused it. A theater camp is not attackable and has no condition (`spawnTheater`); a nest is, at its building level's condition (`spawnLair`). Half of the 160 creature-nest templates carry one child, nearly all of them fog or insects, which is the nest's own effect.
