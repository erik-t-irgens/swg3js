# Assets: where SWG content comes from and what we may use

Findings from research into the SWG client archives, the emulator projects and the file formats, verified where possible against primary sources (the SWGEmu launcher manifests and server config, community launcher manifests, and the leaked SOE engine source used only as a format reference). Nothing here is legal advice.

## The short version

- The 2003 retail discs hold launch-era content only. Almost everything people remember (RIS armor, Mandalorian armor, expansion species, every later clothing and armor set) arrived in later patch archives.
- Later content was always delivered as additional `.tre` archives. The base archives on the discs were never replaced, so a disc install plus the patch archives is the complete client.
- SWGEmu's launcher copies the disc archives from your install and downloads the remaining pre-CU patch archives from the project's mirror. That is the project's own workflow, and its rule is that you must own the discs. You do.
- The NGE-era projects (SWG Legends, SWG Restoration) distribute the entire final client through their launchers. Reports say no retail copy is required, though their pages could not be fetched directly to confirm the wording.
- No cease-and-desist, takedown or lawsuit against any SWG emulator was found in fifteen-plus years. The projects' posture is non-profit, non-affiliated, and (for SWGEmu) own-the-discs.

So for a private, friends-only build: use the discs you own plus the SOE-origin patch archives from an emulator install, convert locally, never commit or host the output, and exclude the emulator projects' own custom archives. That is the same footing the emulators themselves stand on.

## Which archives are yours to convert

Only SOE-origin archives: `bottom.tre`, `default_patch.tre`, `holidays.tre`, `data_*.tre`, `patch_*.tre`, `hotfix_*.tre`. Emulator projects add their own archives containing work they made themselves and license restrictively: `restoration_N.tre` (Restoration), `Stardust_0N.tre`, `mtg_patch_NNN.tre` and `mtg_planets.tre` (ModTheGalaxy), `swgsource_3.0.tre` (SWG Source), and SWG Legends encrypts its client data. Leave all of those out.

`npm run swg -- verify <dir>` hashes every archive in a directory against the retail manifests in `tools/swg/manifests/retail.json` (names, sizes and hashes only, taken from the SWGEmu launcher manifest, a CU launcher manifest and a final-client manifest) and says which are retail. `--retail-only` on any other command mounts only archives whose names and sizes match those manifests.

## How the client resolves files

The client reads `[SharedFile] searchTree_<sku>_<priority>` entries from its config and searches the highest priority first. A zero-length record in an archive is a deletion marker that hides the file from lower archives. The converter models the same order from archive names (bottom, base data, each publish in sequence with expansion archives just above their base patch and hotfixes above that, `default_patch` on top) and honours deletion markers, so `list` and `read` see what the game saw.

## Format facts the converter relies on

- Skies: `terrain/environment/<planet>.iff` is `FORM ENVM` > `FORM 0000` with chunks `NSKY` (night sky shader), `STAR` (colour ramp file, count), `SUN `/`MOON` (shader, size, glow shader, glow size), `SSUN`/`SMOO` (the same plus yaw and pitch in degrees), `CELS` (shader, size, glow shader, glow size, yaw, pitch, pitch direction, cycle time), `TLOK` (uint8 locked, float time), `SKYB` (uint8 cube map, texture name mask) and `DIST`/`PLAN` forms for distant appearances. `datatables/environment/<planet>.iff` is an ordinary datatable read by column index: name, weather index, gradient sky texture, bottom cloud shader/size/speed, top cloud shader/size/speed, colour ramp file, shadows, fog enabled, minimum and maximum fog density, camera appearance, day and night environment textures, seven sound and music names, wind speed scale. Colour ramps are 256-wide 32-bit TGAs, rows ambient, main, specular, fill, bounce, clear, fog, shadow (and back and tangent light when 10 rows); light scales are `4 * (alpha - 128) / 128`. The client keeps its main light 22.5 degrees off the vertical and sweeps its yaw half a turn over the day (the first 70% of the cycle, 06:00 to 22:48) and half a turn over the night.
- Cube map DDS files set `DDSCAPS2_CUBEMAP` (0x200 at offset 112) and store the six faces +X -X +Y -Y +Z -Z, each with its full mip chain before the next face.

- TRE header is 36 bytes: `EERT`, `5000`, file count, TOC offset, TOC compressor, TOC compressed size, name-block compressor, name-block compressed size, name-block uncompressed size. Records are 24 bytes: CRC of the name, length, offset, compressor, compressed length, name offset. Compressor 0 is stored (compressed length may be 0), 1 is retired, 2 is zlib. An MD5 block follows the names.
- From Publish 18 onward (the `_client` / `_shared` split), SOE wrote version `6000` archives: the token reads `EERT6000` and the rest of the header is zero. They are data-only blobs. The client reads them through the `.toc` index files (`sku0_client.toc` and so on), which are what the final client lists in its config instead of archives. Verified on a real install: every archive from patch_18 through hotfix_59 is `6000`.
- A `.toc` index has a 36-byte header (token `TOC `, version `0001`, two compressor bytes, file count, entry block size, name block sizes, archive count, archive name block size), then the archive names, 24-byte entries (compressor, archive index, CRC, name length, offset, length, compressed length) and the file names. The converter mounts self-indexed archives first and then applies each index in SKU order.
- IFF blocks are a tag plus a big-endian length. A FORM's length includes its 4-byte type. Payloads are little-endian.
- Static meshes: `FORM MESH > 0005 > APPR + SPS`. Shader groups carry a `NAME` (shader path) and primitives with an `INFO` (int32 type, bool8 hasIndices, bool8 hasSortedIndices), a `VTXA > 0003 > INFO + DATA` vertex buffer and an `INDX` (int32 count, uint16 indices). Real files use primitive type 9, indexed triangle list.
- Vertex flags: bit 0 position, 1 transformed, 2 normal, 3 color0, 4 color1, 5 point size, bits 8-11 texture set count, two bits per set from bit 12 holding dimension minus one. Fields are stored in that order. Packed colours are ARGB written little-endian, so B, G, R, A on disk.
- The engine is left-handed Y-up. SOE's own Maya exporter negates X, so the converter negates X and reverses winding to produce right-handed GLTF.
- Shaders: `FORM SSHT` (sometimes inside `FORM CSHD`) holds `TXMS` with one `TXM` per slot: `DATA` (uint32 tag, written little-endian so `MAIN` reads `NIAM` on disk, then sampler bytes) and `NAME` (the `.dds` path).
- Textures are plain DDS: DXT1 to DXT5 plus uncompressed masked formats.
- Flora (`FGRP` inside `TGEN`): `FORM FGRP > FORM 0008 > FFAM` chunks, one per family: id, name, colour (3 bytes), density, floats flag, child count, then per child the appearance file, weight, sway (flag, displacement, period), align-to-terrain flag, scale (flag, minimum, maximum). Versions 6 and 7 lack the later fields. The static flora affectors `AFSC` (collidable) and `AFSN` (non-collidable) carry family id, operation, remove-all flag and a density override; they mark poles with a family when a random number seeded by the pole's coordinates (`CoordinateHash` of the two floats, through a Park-Miller generator) is below the family density. Version 15 terrain files also bake the collidable flora per 16 m tile after the layers: `PIMP` (a packed integer map: width, height, bits per element, minimum; elements packed LSB first) and `PFPM` (a packed fixed-point map: a `PIMP` plus a resolution) for the family id and ground height of each tile.
- Flora placement: one collidable plant per 16 m tile at a position, heading and scale drawn from `RandomGenerator(tileKey)` (key = row-major tile index with the map centre as origin), the tile border from the terrain header, the family from the baked map (or the generated chunk for older files), the child by weight from `CoordinateHash(floor x, floor z)`. Non-collidable plants use the header's non-collidable tile size and border, the generated chunk's map, and heading x + z.
- Water: the terrain header's global water table (flag, height, shader size, shader) and, per rectangle or polygon boundary, a local water table (flag, global flag, height, shader size, water type, shader). Rivers (`ARIV`) can carry water tables too; not rendered yet.
- Client regions (`datatables/clientregion/<planet>.iff`): a datatable (`FORM DTII > 0001 > COLS, TYPE, ROWS`) of string id, x, z, radius; names resolve through `string/en/<table>.stf` (magic 0xabcd, version, next id, count, entries of id, crc, length, UTF-16LE text, then names).
- Buildout areas: `datatables/buildout/areas_<planet>.iff` lists areas (`area`, `x1`, `z1`, `x2`, `z2`, `eventRequired`, ...); `datatables/buildout/<planet>/<area>.iff` lists objects (`objid`, `container`, `shared_template_crc`, `cell_index`, `px py pz`, `qw qx qy qz`, `radius`, `portal_layout_crc`, optional `sx sy sz`). Objects outside cells are relative to the area's `x1, z1`; objects in cells are relative to their cell, whose row (template `object/cell/shared_cell.iff`) is contained by the building. Older tables without `objid` assign containment by order: a building row, its cell rows, then their contents. `misc/object_template_crc_string_table.iff` (`FORM CSTB > 0000 > DATA count, CRCT uint32 crcs, STRT int32 offsets, STNG strings`) names the templates.
- Particle effects (`.prt`): `FORM PEFT > 000N > [PTIM] 0000(int32 groups; v2: initial playback rate, its time, playback rate, scale) EMGP...`; `EMGP > 000N > [PTIM] 0000(int32 emitters) EMTR...`; `EMTR > 00NN` (versions 0 to 14) `> [PTIM] 16 x WVFM 0000(emitter fields) PTQD|PTMH`. The 16 waveforms are translation x y z, rotation x y z (turns), distance, shape size, spread (degrees), generation rate, emit speed, inherited velocity, cluster count and radius, particle life, weight. `WVFM > 000N`: int32 interpolation, [v1: int32 sample type], [v2: float min, max], int32 count, then (percent, value, random a, random b) with a/b written max-then-min before version 2. `CLRR` (colour ramps) is the same shape with r g b per point. `PTQD > [PTCL] 000N > WVFM rotation, length, width (half extents), PTEX(shader, frame count, start, end, uv size, frames per column, frames per second with -1 meaning over the life, visible), [v1: 0000 linked flag]`; `PTCL > 000N > 0000(name, random rotation direction, [v2: attachment count]) CLRR colour, WVFM alpha, speed scale, [v3: relative rotation x y z], PATT...`. The emitter fields chunk grows by version: name from v3, particle type from v4, sound from v5, local space from v6, generation type, one-shot counts, ground collision and the velocity kept after a bounce from v7, kill on collision from v8, emitter life max and LOD distances from v9, flocking from v10, time-of-day colour from v11, the flocking cage and snap-to-terrain from v12, first-particle-immediately from v13, priority from v14.
- Skeletal appearances: `.sat` (`FORM SMAT > 000N > INFO`(mesh count, skeleton count, controller flag) `MSGN`(mesh generator files) `SKTI`(skeleton file, attachment joint) `LATX`(skeleton to `.lat` map)); `.lmg` (`MLOD > 0000 > INFO`(count) `NAME` per level); `.mgn` (`SKMG > 0004 > INFO`(max transforms per vertex and shader, skeleton, transform, position, weight, normal, shader and blend-target counts, occlusion counts as int16) `SKTM`(skeleton files) `XFNM`(joint names) `POSN`(positions) `TWHD`(weights per position) `TWDT`(joint index, weight) `NORM` `DOT3` `HPTS` `BLTS`(morph targets) `OZN `(zone names)/`FOZC`(uint16 count, int16 zone indices: the mesh is hidden when all are occluded)/`OZC `(per combination: int16 count, int16 zone indices)/`ZTO `(int16 zone indices this mesh occludes on meshes beneath it; the header's last int16 is the mesh's occlusion layer, outermost highest) then `PSDT` per shader: `NAME` `PIDX`(position indices) `NIDX` `DOT3` `VDCL` `TXCI`(set count and dimensions) `TCSF > TCSD` `PRIM > INFO + ITL /OITL`(triangles; OITL rows carry an int16 zone combination first: the triangle is hidden when every zone of that combination is occluded by a higher layer)); `.skt` (`SKTM > 0002 > INFO`(count) `NAME` `PRNT` `RPRE` `RPST` `BPTR` `BPRO` `JROR`); `.lat` (`LATT > 0000 > INFO`(hierarchy file, count) then `ANIM > INFO`(logical name) + an animation template form, `KFAT` inline or `PXAT > 0000 > INFO`(target `.ans`)); `.ans` (`KFAT > 0003 > INFO`(fps, frames, counts) or the compressed `CKAT > 0001` with int16 counts, uint8 translation masks and rotations packed into 32 bits: x and y in 11 bits and z in 10, each a sign bit plus an offset from a base chosen by a per-channel format byte whose high bits set the precision level and low bits the base, w recovered from unit length; `XFRM > XFIN`(joint, animated-rotation flag, rotation index, translation mask 0x08/0x10/0x20 for x/y/z, per-axis indices) `AROT > QCHN`(keys: frame, w x y z) `SROT`(static quaternions) `ATRN > CHNL`(keys: frame, value) `STRN`(static floats) `LOCT`/`LOCR`(locomotion)). Quaternions are stored w x y z and multiply as Hamilton products; a joint's local rotation is post × (animation × bind) × pre, its translation bind + animation. Three-letter tags are space-padded (`ITL `).
- World snapshots (`.ws`): `FORM WSNP > 0001 > NODS` of `NODE` forms plus an `OTNL` chunk of template names. Each node's `DATA` holds network id, container id, template index, cell index, a quaternion (w, x, y, z), a position, a radius and the portal layout CRC; child nodes are objects inside the building's cells.
- Object templates: `FORM <TYPE> > [DERV { base template }] > FORM 00NN > PCNT + one chunk per parameter` (name, then for strings an int8 type byte of 1 and the value). `appearanceFilename` is inherited through the base chain.
- Portal objects (`.pob`): `FORM PRTO > 000N > CELS > CELL` forms; cell 0 is the exterior and its `DATA` names the appearance.
- Terrain templates (`.trn`): `FORM PTAT > 0013..0015 > DATA + TGEN + BAKE (+ flora maps)`. The header holds the name, map width, chunk width, tiles per chunk, the global water table and flora sampling parameters. `TGEN > 0000` carries the shader (`SGRP`), flora, radial, environment, fractal (`MGRP`: `MFAM` families with an `MFRC` record of seed, bias, gain, octaves, frequency, amplitude, scale, offset and combination rule) and bitmap groups, then `LYRS` of `LAYR` layers. Every layer item is `FORM <TAG> > FORM 00NN > IHDR (active flag, name) + DATA`; layers add an `ADTA` chunk (invert boundaries, invert filters) and nest freely. Boundaries are `BCIR`, `BREC`, `BPOL`, `BPLN`; filters `FHGT`, `FFRA`, `FSLP`, `FDIR`, `FSHD`, `FBIT`; height affectors `AHCN`, `AHFR`, `AHTR`, `AROA` (roads) and `ARIV` (rivers, with baked `HDTA` centre-line heights); the rest colour, flora and shaders.
- Terrain bitmaps: the second `MGRP` block in `TGEN` is the bitmap group (`MFAM > DATA`: family id, name, TGA path). `FBIT` filters stretch that 8-bit greyscale image over the owning layer's boundary extent, sample it bilinearly with rows running bottom-up in world Z, and multiply the feathered range test by the value, so an affector under them applies in proportion to the pixel. Mos Eisley's worn ground is one of these: a rectangle over the city, a bitmap, and a 2 m subtract. The converter decodes the TGA (uncompressed or RLE, greyscale, colour-mapped or true colour) into `terrain/<name>.hmap` (`HMAP`, width, height, top-down bytes).
- Terrain generation: a chunk is a grid of poles every half tile, padded by two poles on each side; each layer rasterises its boundaries into an amount map (feathered, optionally inverted), narrows it with its filters, applies its affectors with that amount, and passes the amount map to its sub-layers. The client's ground is a fan of eight triangles around each tile's centre pole, which is what the height sampler reproduces.
- Building terrain modifications: a building template's `terrainModificationFileName` names a `.lay` file (the five group forms followed by a `LAYR`). The client moves its circle and rectangle boundaries to the building, yaws the rectangles by the building's heading, sets every height constant to the ground height at the building's centre, and appends the layer to the generator. Polygons in such files stay where they are.
- Texture renderers: `.trt` (`FORM BTRT > 0001|0002 > INFO`(owner) `DEST`(width, height, runtime format count and formats) `SHTM`(`INFO` count, then a `NAME` chunk per shader file or an inline `SSHT`/`CSHD` form) `TXTS > INFO`(count, texture file names) `VBS `(`INFO` count, `VTXA` vertex buffers: `000N > INFO`(uint32 format flags: position 1, transformed 2, normal 4, colour0 8, colour1 16, point size 32, coordinate-set count in bits 8-11, each set's dimension minus one in two bits from bit 12; vertex count) `DATA`) `IBS `(`INFO` count, `IDAT`(count, uint16 indices)) `CAM > PCAM`(parallel projection length: x and y run 0..length across the destination, y down) `RCMS`(`INFO` count, then `CFBC`(clear colour flag, ARGB, depth flag, depth, stencil flag, stencil) or `SRSC`(`INFO` shader index and count, `RTLI`(vertex buffer, index buffer, minimum index, vertex count, start index, triangle count) or `RTFC`(vertex buffer, drawn as a fan))) `PCMS`(`INFO` count, `PCMD` forms: optional `COND` (`TRUE`, `FALS`, `CVTP` deprecated and true, `OR  `, `AND `), `TOPS`/`FOPS`(`INFO` count, operations `NOP `, `SSTC`(shader, tag, texture), `SST1`(shader, tag, base texture, variable name, count), `SST2`(shader, tag, base, name0, count0, name1, count1), `STF `(shader, tag, variable), `STFA`(shader, tag, r g b, alpha variable), `STFP`(int16 shader, tag, palette file, variable name, private flag)))). Variables are registered in load order, by name; ranged ints default to their minimum and palette colours to entry 0. Tags are stored as little-endian uint32 (`MAIN` on disk is `NIAM`).
- Terrain shader families (`SGRP > 000N > SFAM`: id, name, [surface name], three colour bytes, shader size in metres per repeat, [v3 float], feather clamp, child count, then child shader files with weights) are painted onto the pole grid by the shader affectors (`ASCN` constant, `ASRP` replace, road and river affectors); a chunk's ground texture is the family at each pole. The game blends up to three families per triangle at the corners' families with barycentric weights, sampling each family's texture at world position divided by its shader size.
- Palettes: `.pal` are Microsoft RIFF palettes (`RIFF` size `PAL ` `data` size, uint8 zero, uint8 version 3, uint16 count, entries r g b flags). Skin, hair and eye colours are palette indices in the customization data; the shader multiplies a greyscale base by the chosen entry through a texture factor.
- Static shaders: `.sht` (`FORM SSHT > 0000|0001`: `MATS`, `TXMS > TXM > 000N > DATA`(v0: placeholder flag, tag; v1/v2: tag, placeholder flag, address u v w, mip/min/mag filters, v2 anisotropy) + `NAME`(texture file), `TCSS > 0000`(tag, coordinate set index), `TFNS > 0000`(tag, ARGB texture factor), `TSNS`, `ARVS > 0000`(tag, alpha test reference), `SRVS`, `NAME`(effect file)); `CSHD > 0000|0001` wraps a base shader (`NAME` or inline) with `MATR`(material colours from palettes), `TXTR > DATA`(int16 count, texture files) + `CUST > TX1D`(tag, base index, count, variable, private flag, default), `TFAC > PAL `(variable, private flag, tag, palette, default index)). Effects: `.eff` (`EFCT > 000N > DATA`(implementation count, ...) then `IMPL` forms or `NAME` chunks; `IMPL > 000N > [SCAP](capability level) [OPTN] DATA`(pass count, phase, ...) `PASS > 000N > DATA`(stage count and render state: alpha blend enable/operation/source/destination, alpha test enable/reference tag/function, colour write mask, texture factor tag(s)) then `PFFP` or `PVSH` and `STAG > 0000|0001`(colour operation and three arguments with complement and alpha-replicate flags, alpha operation and arguments, result register, texture tag, coordinate set tag) or `PPSH`). Operations and arguments follow Direct3D 8 texture stage states (select, modulate, add, blend by diffuse/texture/factor/current alpha, dot3, multiply-add, lerp; current, diffuse, specular, temp, texture, texture factor).
- Sound templates (`.snd`, 7,171 of them: 3,115 `SD2D` and 4,056 `SD3D`): `FORM SD2D > 0003`, or `FORM SD3D > [FORM SD2D > 0003] + 0001` where the `0001` chunk is always empty; 528 files (all voice-over or tutorial lines, all `SD2D`) are followed by a stray empty `0001` chunk at the top level, so a reader must take the first root form. The `0003` chunk is an int32 sample count, that many C-string sample paths, then exactly 28 four-byte fields (112 bytes) in every one of the 7,171 files: 0-1 start delay min and max, 2-3 fade in, 4-5 loop count (−1 or 99 for ever), 6-7 gap between loops, 8-9 fade out, 10 category (0 ambient, 1 explosion, 2 item, 3 movement, 4 interface, 5 vehicle, 6 vocalization, 7 weapon, 8 background music, 9 player music, 10 machine, 13 voice-over), 11 play order (0 random, 2 in order), 12 always 0, 13 loop-gap mode, 14-15 fade modes, 16-20 volume (mode, drift period, min, max, glide), 21-25 the same for pitch with the range in semitones, 26 priority (0 highest), 27 the metres within which the sound plays at full volume. Fields 0-10, 18, 19, 23, 24, 26 and 27 are clear from the data; 11 to 17, 20, 22 and 25 are readings. How loudness falls off beyond field 27, the voice limits and the client's audio library's configuration are client code and are not in the archives. Every sample is 16-bit PCM WAV or MP3; 89 WAVs carry a RIFF `smpl` loop chunk and only 3 loop part of the file.
- Client data (`clientdata/**/*.cdf` and `.iff`, `FORM CLDF > 0000`) says what an object does. Sound-bearing chunks: `CEFT`, `CSND` and `EVNT` (an event name then a `.cef` or `.snd` path; three snow and afflicted weapons write a `.prt` as their `explode` event, and five creature voices write a sound with no extension at all), `ASND` (the object's own looping sound), `DSTR > INFO` (the effect on destruction), `FORM DAMA` (a damage state: `INFO` two floats and a client effect, `APPR`, `HARD`, `PSOR`, `ASND`), `FORM VTHR` (a thruster for one damage level: `INFO` one float, `HOBJ` appearances, and `VSND`, a name then eight sound paths: idle, speed-up, slow-down, run, then four sounds of that damage), `FORM VGEF > INFO` (a ground effect: one byte that is 1 over water, then the sound, the effect's name and its appearance), and `FORM ENGS > FORM INTS > INFO` or a top-level `FORM INTS > INFO` (a slot or hardpoint name, a sound, then six undecoded floats: 0.7, 1, −16, 0, 0.3, 0.3 on an undamaged engine and 0.5, 0.8, −16, 0, 0.3, 0.3 on each damage loop). Paths may be written with backslashes and with one or two leading `@` signs (`@@sound/wings_open_xwing.snd`).
- Which file speaks for a ship, a ship part or a vehicle. A part's is named by the look's own attachment template (`object/tangible/ship/attachment/**`, 230 of 441 reaching one, 200 of those naming a sound), never by the component in the slot, which names none at all. A hull's is named by its own template and usually holds nothing but the effect it is destroyed by, so its engine is read from its looks table's `engine` column instead (`datatables/space/ship_chassis_<chassis>.iff`, the modal cell, as the stock parts are picked), which is the only answer for a hull whose engines are part of its mesh. A vehicle's is named by nothing in the object templates -- one of the 63 names a file and the archives do not hold it -- so it is reached through `datatables/mount/logical_saddle_name_map.iff` and then `saddle_appearance_map.iff`, whose `client_data_filename` column is filled on 56 of its 68 rows and joins all 60 vehicles. A top-level `FORM INTS` is the booster's loop at a hardpoint rather than the ship's interior: all 53 in the archives name the same rocket loop, on the 46 booster parts and on the eight hulls whose booster is part of the hull. `datatables/space/ship_power_sounds.iff`'s three rows differ in exactly one pair of columns, the generic, Alliance and Imperial chime; which hull read which row was the client's and is not in the archives.
- Client effects (`clienteffect/*.cef`, `FORM CLEF > 0001`), 1,037 files, all of which parse: `PSND` a sound (854), `CPAP` a particle effect (639), `CAMS` a camera shake (193), `CLGT` a light (119), `FFBK` force feedback (73). The tables that place sounds: `datatables/interior/interior.iff` (265 rows over 166 buildings: `PobName`, `Cell Name`, day and night beds, first music, `Surface Type`, fog, environment map, `Room Type`), `datatables/player/sounds.iff` (31 interface sounds), `datatables/weapon/combat_effects_melee.iff` and `combat_effects_ranged.iff`, `datatables/appearance/door_style.iff` (117 styles, of which 114 carry a client effect: 112 carry only the open-begin and close-end of the four, one carries three and one carries all four), `datatables/space/ship_power_sounds.iff`, `ship_hit_sounds.iff` (whose fallback row has an empty type where the power table spells `default`) and `ship_chassis.iff`'s `flyby_sound` and `hit_sound_group`. `scene/*.iff` names sounds too: `hyperspace.iff` the jump's three stage sounds and the two `game_music_manager*` files the combat score. `abstract/terrain_surface/<name>.iff` (`FORM STER`) is an ordinary object template whose `surfaceType` string and `cover` float say what is under a foot; a float parameter carries the same delta byte between its int8 type and its value that an integer one does.
- What a shot sounds like is spread over two kinds of table. `datatables/weapon/weapon.iff` has 70 rows and fifteen client-effect columns apiece: the fire, the hit on a creature, on metal, on stone, on wood and on anything else, the miss in water, on terrain, on a creature, on metal, on stone, on wood, on anything else and on nothing at all, and the ricochet. 1,044 of those 1,050 cells are filled, every filled one names a `.cef` the archives hold, and every one of those names exactly one sound (1,002) or none (42), never two. Several columns usually name one effect (a light blaster's metal, stone, wood and other are all its "other" effect), so what is really heard is a creature against everything else, plus the water, the ground and nothing. `datatables/weapon/combat_effects_ranged.iff` is the other kind: 271 rows, 267 keyed on a template's file name (`shared_carbine_cdef.iff`) and four on a kind (`pistol`, `lightRifle`, `rifle`, `heavyWeapon`), 270 naming a muzzle sound and 129 a `Hit Target Sound`, which is the blow on what was aimed at and not on any surface; not one row names a miss or a ricochet. `combat_effects_melee.iff` has 133 rows, every one naming a swing and only six a hit (three of them the lightsabers').
- Where a world puts its sounds: `object/soundobject/*` (86 templates) are objects placed for their sound alone, and their sound — like any prop's own hum — is the `ASND` of the client data their `clientDataFile` names, taken up the template chain. The snapshots and buildout tables place 491 of them, on Corellia, Naboo, Tatooine, Rori, Lok, Talus, Dantooine and Dathomir only; 3,623 other placed objects hum the same way. A placed object inside a building is contained by an `object/cell/shared_cell.iff` node, not by the building: that node's `cellIndex` is the room (cell 0 is the exterior) and its transform is always identity, and the cell node in turn is contained by the building, whose `portalLayoutFilename` names the `.pob`. The object's own `cellIndex` is usually 0, but where it is set (130 of Tatooine's 5,102 objects inside a cell, 428 of Naboo's 778) it always agrees with its container's, so either answers. The room table's `PobName` is that file's name without its folder or its `.pob`. An ordinary object template's `surfaceType` is an int, not a string: 0 other (the resting value, on 765 of the 1,170 templates Tatooine places), 1 metal, 2 stone, 3 wood, 4 acid, 5 ice, 6 molten, 7 obsidian, the upper four fixed by the asteroid templates. Metal, stone and wood, the only three a planet ever places, are also names a body's client data steps with (`footstep_<surface>`); there is no `footstep_acid`, `_ice`, `_molten` or `_obsidian` in any of the 6,152 client data files.
- When things happen inside an animation: `appearance/animation/*.ans` (`FORM CKAT > 0001` or `FORM KFAT > 0003`) may hold a `FORM MSGS` beside its keyframes -- an `INFO` chunk (int16 message count), then one `MESG` per message: an int16 frame count, a C-string name, then that many int16 frame numbers. 3,308 of the 8,351 retail animations carry one and every one parses to the byte (the declared count equals the chunks, and each message ends exactly on its last frame number). The clip's own rate and frame count are in the version form's `INFO` (a float rate, then an int32 frame count in `KFAT` and an int16 in `CKAT`), read without decoding a keyframe channel. 120 distinct names, the biggest being `event_footstep` (1,342 clips), `fire1..fire11` (3,857 between them), `event_vocalize` (310), `event_hitlight` (255), `event_hitground` (253), `event_hitheavy` (221) and `event_flap` (195). No frame number is past its clip's count, but 1,524 sit exactly on it, which is one past the last keyframe: a clip's frames are numbered 0 to count − 1. No `.lat` entry anywhere is an inline animation: over all 190 retail tables 14,212 of 14,213 entries are `.ans` file references and the last is `none` (114 of those references name an `.ans` no archive holds). Jedi Academy's own marks are text, not IFF: `models/players/_humanoid/animevents.cfg` is an `UPPEREVENTS` and a `LOWEREVENTS` block of one line each (the animation, the kind, the frame, then that kind's fields), 519 lines in all, and the frame is counted from the animation's own first frame in `animation.cfg`, not from the shared `.gla`; a sound path beginning with a star is the character's own voice set rather than a file, and `ext_data/sabers/*.sab` is a name, a brace block and one `key value` a line (17 files, 30 hilts, 24 of which name a hum). A line's last field is a chance out of a hundred in which nought means always, not a fraction: of the 519 lines 516 carry nought and three carry 30.

## Reference implementations

Open-source readers that agree with the engine: SWGEmu Core3 `MMOCoreORB/src/tre3`, Kenneth Sewell's treLib, the `swg-rs` crate, Swg.Explorer (C#), and nostyleguy's Blender addon `io_scene_swg_msh`. The SWG-Titan/CSRC repository mirrors the leaked SOE engine source and is the authoritative description of every loader, but it is leaked proprietary code: read it to understand formats, never copy it. One warning: the Blender addon's `vertex_buffer_format.py` defines the point-size flag as 0x10 instead of 0x20, and the CSRC repo's own `swg_blender_addon/formats/msh.py` does not match retail files.
